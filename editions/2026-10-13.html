7. Europe buys time on Chinese cars and argues about how to spend it

Brussels and Beijing reached an understanding that shields the hybrid segment from Chinese imports, which European carmakers welcomed as a first step towards a fairer field while other industries warned that China's trade surplus will not move without more. The respite is read as temporary even by the people who asked for it, and Macron says a Franco-German deal on car policy is imminent that would push back the combustion-engine phaseout and lift production on the continent. What the truce defers is the structural choice: extract real concessions on the imbalance, or reach for unilateral instruments to protect the industrial base. On the demand side the Industrial Accelerator Act is meant to tie public spending to European and low-carbon production, but as drafted it would cover only a small share of purchases in most sectors and its exemptions let governments opt out too easily. Philipp Jäger and Nils Redeker put the workable landing zone at two moves: keep the regime open to trade partners where fair competition and supply security allow while hardening anti-circumvention rules, and give local-content and low-carbon requirements enough bite to pull manufacturing investment.
