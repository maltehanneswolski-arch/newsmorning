Trump talks Nvidia's board into taking Huang back

Nvidia's board voted unanimously on Monday to reinstate Jensen Huang, 11 days after he resigned over an affair with Alice Huang, whom US intelligence ties to China's spy agency; Trump told CNBC he made the deal, and the shares rose 31 percent. Hours later Commerce put Megaspeed, the Singapore-based Nvidia partner under investigation since 2024 for diverting chips to China, and its Thai and Malaysian units on a trade blacklist. Republicans joined Democrats in demanding answers, which raises the odds of the MATCH Act and the Remote Access Security Act around the G20 in Miami, on top of the 16 November BIS rule requiring a licence for remote access to advanced compute by China-headquartered users.

Berlin takes a Russian plan for a nuclear satellite seriously

Berlin is taking seriously intelligence shared by western allies that Moscow could launch a nuclear-armed Sputnik-S satellite as soon as next month, within 60 days; WDR, NDR and the Süddeutsche Zeitung, citing German military and BND sources, report a weapon able to destroy other satellites. The Kremlin calls the reports propaganda, and the government sees no immediate threat but is alarmed, a week after Putin, Xi and Trump pledged in writing in Shenzhen never to use nuclear weapons. NATO's Mark Rutte said the alliance would defend against threats in space.

Merz's pension deadline runs into the SPD congress

Lars Klingbeil opens budget week in the Bundestag on Tuesday with net borrowing of €122.9 billion in the 2027 core budget and about €210 billion with the special funds, plus off-budget bookings that Jens Boysen-Hogrefe of the Kiel Institute calls historically high. Pensions are the harder fight: Merz has promised cabinet approval of the package this year, but the SPD now wants to keep penalty-free retirement after 45 years, which the pension commission proposed abolishing, while the Union would keep it only for those unable to work. Klingbeil, seeking re-election at the SPD congress in Bremen in mid-January, has little room to concede; Wiebke Winter puts the federal pension subsidy at about €340 million a day.

Farm money survives the cuts in the EU budget draft

Leaders meet on Thursday over the 2028-2034 budget, and Saturday's revised draft raises the earmark for the Common Agricultural Policy while every other line is cut; the Commission opened talks with a 25 percent CAP cut, and Ursula von der Leyen has pledged €45 billion to sweeten the Mercosur deal for farmers. France's agriculture minister Annie Genevard still calls the farm envelope largely inadequate, and tractors are due in Brussels for Thursday's protests. As Visegrad Four and Austrian leaders attacked the draft, Hungary's Péter Magyar said he did not consider the proposal a basis for negotiations, and he rejects tying the budget to continued support for Ukraine.

Burnham rules out rejoining the EU without a referendum

Andy Burnham said on Monday it would be unthinkable for Britain to rejoin the EU without another referendum, his firmest commitment yet to asking voters if he concludes the UK should go back in. London sanctions Myanmar's generals after the genocide ruling

Fourteen countries from Europe, Africa and Oceania acknowledged the ICJ ruling that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, the first time the court has found a state guilty of genocide, and the UK sanctioned five senior military figures and said it would formally recognise the genocide. The ruling covers the 2017 Rakhine campaign that killed at least 10,000 people. Malaysia, despite acknowledging the junta's atrocities, plans to send more than 6,500 migrants back to Myanmar in December.
