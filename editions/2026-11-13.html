Person

Ola Källenius

Sweden · b. 1969

Chairman of the Board of Management of Mercedes-Benz Group

A Swede who joined Daimler-Benz in 1993 and ran Mercedes-AMG and later group research and development, he became chief executive in May 2019, the first non-German to lead the company.

Why it matters. Mercedes has frozen all new investment in Germany and threatened plant closures unless IG Metall accepts a 38-hour week at unchanged pay and cuts that would save about €800 million a year.

Daimler split off its truck business in 2021 and renamed itself Mercedes-Benz Group in 2022 under his leadership.

IG Metall

Industriegewerkschaft Metall · Frankfurt am Main

Germany's largest trade union, covering the metal, electrical and car industries

It bargains sector-wide wage agreements for the metal and electrical industries and sits on the supervisory boards of the big carmakers under German co-determination law.

Why it matters. It is holding Mercedes-Benz to the 35-hour week that the carmaker now wants to stretch to 38 hours without extra pay.

A seven-week strike in 1984 began the path to the 35-hour week, which the western metal industry reached in 1995.

Europe wins a seat at the Ukraine talks without a speaker

Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff left Moscow on Thursday saying Europeans will join the next round of Russia–Ukraine talks; Kushner says Putin is willing to try, though Moscow has not formally agreed and Putin refused to soften his territorial demands and warned he would target any foreign troops sent to Ukraine. In Dublin the EU has no answer to who would take that seat. Giorgia Meloni wants one negotiator because only the E3 (Britain, France and Germany) have been in the room so far, while Emmanuel Macron and Friedrich Merz say a common position matters more than a name. António Costa calls for a team effort by Europe, the EU and NATO; leaders return to it on the second day of the summit on Friday.

Merz makes the Ukraine loan the price of the EU budget

Friedrich Merz has told António Costa in writing that there will be no 2028–2034 budget unless every member state backs Ukraine, using Germany's weight as the largest net contributor against Slovakia, Czechia and Hungary, which depend on EU funds. Robert Fico calls it blackmail, and Péter Magyar and Andrej Babiš are also critical. The 2027 Ukraine loan is set at €70bn, €45bn of it from the EU, and up to €10bn of unused SAFE defence money could lower the burden. Costa wants the budget settled at the December summit, while Gitanas Nausėda says a deal by 4 February is soon enough.

Mercedes freezes investment in Germany

Mercedes-Benz has stopped all new investment in Germany and says plant closures and moves abroad are on the table unless staff work 38 hours a week instead of 35 at the same pay and give up parts of their Christmas pay, holiday pay and profit share, saving about €800m a year. Labour costs are 17.9% lower at its plant in Tuscaloosa, Alabama, and 75% lower in Kecskemét, Hungary, now its largest European plant; IG Metall is holding to the 35-hour week. BMW, Mercedes and Volkswagen are expected to earn €11bn together in 2026, against €50bn in 2021, and their share of DAX profits falls from about 40% to 9%.
