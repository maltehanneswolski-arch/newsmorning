Emmanuel Macron

France · b. 1977

President of the French Republic

Former investment banker and economy minister who won the presidency in 2017 and again in 2022 and cannot stand a third consecutive time. His second term has run without an absolute majority in the National Assembly.

Why it matters. He addressed the country on Sunday evening after the worst street violence in years, conceded the anger behind the marches and refused his interior minister's resignation within the same day. Six months from the election that replaces him, every decision he takes about policing also decides who inherits the argument.

US Ambassador Andrew Puzder warned of devastating retaliation against European companies if Brussels goes ahead with plans to limit spectrum access for non-EU operators, a restriction whose practical target is Starlink. A record datacenter pipeline runs into the first serious AI sell-off

Nasdaq 100 futures rose 1.3 per cent on Monday after the biggest weekly drop since the April 2025 tariff shock, and the reversal travelled east: Seoul opened 3.17 per cent down before trimming the loss to 1.69 per cent, with the KOSPI at 5,572.10 by 9:15 a.m. The selling is driven by one question, whether the money going into AI infrastructure returns enough to justify itself. The construction data answer in the other direction: US hyperscalers have never started more datacenter building, more than 24GW is in advanced construction for 2027 delivery across just 40 campuses, and the forecast is above 50GW added in a single year, roughly the entire global stock built through 2024. The binding constraint is migrating off power and land toward memory and logic supply in 2027-28, which is where Korean and Taiwanese capacity decides the ceiling rather than American grid connections. Dollar funding is tightening at the same time, pushed by the Iran war and by shifts in Chinese behaviour, and Thailand is already discovering that attracting the big-ticket facility is easier than building the domestic capability around it.

Exports carry China to 4.5 per cent, and Beijing refuses the word overcapacity

Third-quarter growth came in at 4.5 per cent, up from a more-than-three-year low of 4.3 per cent in April to June and ahead of forecasts, which puts the full-year target within reach and lets policymakers hold off on further easing. The composition is the whole story: a prolonged housing crash, sluggish consumer spending and plunging investment leave exports, lifted by AI-related demand, doing the work. At the China Outbound Investment Forum in Beijing on 17 October, former commerce minister Chen Deming argued the imbalance should be described as strong supply and weak demand rather than overcapacity, because the second term assigns responsibility for global surpluses and deficits to Chinese production alone. He said Beijing still owes its firms easier terms abroad, above all investment agreements with host countries, and framed outbound investment as the way to raise consumer demand elsewhere rather than as a way to dump output. That is the argument EU trade-defence cases will meet this winter, now made in Beijing by the people who used to sign the files.

A bridge opens on the Yalu as North Korean trade with China hits 299 million dollars

North Korea's trade with China reached $299 million in September, up from $238 million in August and the second-highest monthly figure this year, according to China's General Administration of Customs, and it lands as the two open their largest cross-border bridge after twelve years of construction. The bridge converts a political relationship into fixed capacity: throughput across the Yalu stops depending on which crossing Beijing chooses to staff on a given month, and the leverage that came with that choice transfers from Moscow and Washington to Beijing. Pyongyang's hard-currency base is wider than the customs line suggests, with more than 100,000 overseas workers in at least 17 countries producing between $450 million and $800 million a year on CSIS figures. State-backed upscale barbecue restaurants with terraces, modern ventilation and seating for hundreds are opening at pace, set against persistent food insecurity and unequal access to it. A sanctions architecture designed around a narrow, interruptible channel is now looking at a permanent one.
