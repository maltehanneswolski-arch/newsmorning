Costa's budget box cuts deep and revives a digital tax

Antonio Costa's negotiating box for the 2028-2034 multiannual financial framework (MFF) trims the Commission's plan by about 10 percent, €204bn on EURACTIV's count and €44bn in POLITICO's alert, with competitiveness and external action cut hardest and farm and regional money largely spared. To ease national contributions it revives a 3% tax on digital firms with at least €50m of EU digital turnover and €750m worldwide. Frugal states find the cuts too shallow, the friends of cohesion find no landing zone and France has already called the plan inadequate before leaders meet in Brussels on Thursday; even trimmed, the FT puts it about a quarter above current spending.

Trump's envoys put European security on the Ukraine agenda

Back in Washington, Trump defended the Shenzhen trilateral with Putin and Xi, which yielded a declaration of peace and little else days after he gave the Quad short shrift (Geopolitical Dispatch, Le Monde). The part that reaches Europe is procedural: Trump's envoys have floated broader European security arrangements as a topic for the Ukraine talks, Moscow has revived its pre-war demands, and Kyiv and its allies fear the format weakens NATO (The Hindu). Zelensky called Putin a killer after Trump called him a friend, while Xi left Shenzhen holding a fragile trade truce where he wants it (Japan Times).

The Nvidia smuggling trail runs through Singapore shell companies

The FT ties Alice Huang, the former Megaspeed executive in a relationship with ex-Nvidia chief Jensen Huang, to gaming firms, figures in the Prince Group (an alleged Cambodian criminal network) and hundreds of shell companies moving Nvidia chips to China; the WSJ names her associate Li Ming, charged with fraud in Singapore in 2025, as central to the trade. The NYT says China's Ministry of State Security was hunting AI chips years earlier, and California's probe now covers possible crimes under state law. Commerce's 16 November rule bars offshore data centres with US chips from serving two dozen countries and puts shipments to Malaysia, Thailand and Singapore under licence; Bessent says no further curbs are needed.

Trump's GAO nominee would audit spending he helped withhold

The lame-duck Senate fast-tracked a confirmation hearing for Eric Ueland, Trump's nominee to head the Government Accountability Office (GAO), Congress's 3,200-strong audit arm and the only body with clear authority to sue the president on its behalf. The office is meant to rule on whether the administration broke budget law by refusing to spend money Congress appropriated, a policy Ueland probably helped run. Confirmation would leave Democratic plans to reclaim the power of the purse looking premature. (The Bulwark)

London and Brussels sign three reset agreements

The UK and the EU signed three agreements at a Brussels summit on Friday: a shared sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) area to cut paperwork on food exports, linked emissions trading schemes, and a youth scheme letting tens of thousands of young people live and work across the Channel. Brussels sources had suggested the wider deal would wait until fees fall for EU students at British universities; foreign secretary Ed Miliband says it does not depend on that. Andy Burnham calls Brexit an obvious failure, and the FT expects his plan to keep the next stage to informal brainstorming to run into Le Pen.

A Ukrainian drone sets a Russian freighter alight off Finland

A Ukrainian drone hit a Kaliningrad-bound Russian cargo ship in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday; it caught fire and drifted into Finnish waters, and France 24 links the vessel to a major Russian military logistics company. Petteri Orpo said the war was spilling over into Finland's immediate vicinity and that Ukraine has the right to fight back; hours later a Russian helicopter was suspected of violating Finnish airspace, and Helsinki reimposed temporary air and sea restrictions in the eastern gulf. A Russian ballistic missile killed two people in Zelensky's hometown the same day.

Iran hits Bahrain before a run at the US blockade

Iran's Revolutionary Guards (IRGC) say they hit US military positions at al-Riffa and Awali; Bahrain reports a fire at a civilian industrial site and no injuries, ending a lull in a war that began with US-Israeli strikes in late February, and three tankers were struck in Hormuz.
