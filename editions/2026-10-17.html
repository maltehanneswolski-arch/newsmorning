Person 🇪🇺 🇸🇰

Maroš Šefčovič

Slovakia · b. 1966

European Commissioner for Trade and Economic Security

Career Slovak diplomat who has sat in every Commission since 2009 across energy, interinstitutional relations and the Brexit files, and led the EU side on the Windsor Framework. Trade defence, the anti-coercion instrument and economic-security screening are his to propose, and he is the Commission's most practised negotiator of politically impossible files.

Why it matters. The hybrid-vehicle understanding with China's commerce ministry, which would cap imports at 350,000 vehicles a year, is his file, and he is the one who put a number on it: more than half the projected exports removed over four years. The kill switch leaders asked for by year end would land on the same desk, so the hard instrument and the negotiated one are both his.

One of the longest continuously serving members of the European Commission, having outlasted three of its presidents.

Institution 🇨🇳

MOFCOM

Ministry of Commerce · 商务部

Trade policy, trade remedies and export licensing

Runs anti-dumping and anti-subsidy investigations, administers dual-use and critical-mineral export licences, and maintains the unreliable-entity list. Its measures publish as numbered announcements that take effect on the date of issue, so a trade action can land with no notice period at all.

Why it matters. It is the ministry that reached the hybrid-vehicle understanding with the Commission, the one that would administer any rare-earth licensing attached to it, and the one that answers if the single-market kill switch is ever used. Brussels has now asked for an instrument that could shut Chinese goods out of the single market, and the answer to it would be designed here.

Export licensing for rare earths sits here, which means a European factory's supply can depend on a single administrative decision in Beijing.

A speed cap on Kyiv's satellites becomes a European procurement question

A restriction imposed without warning early on 15 October cuts the data link whenever a Starshield terminal moves faster than 160 kph, which is ordinary speed for the drones Ukraine flies with one fitted. Kyiv had just closed Operation Vivaldi, a Donbas counter-offensive it says recovered 277 square kilometres and which ran on video relayed by satellite to the operators steering those drones. Zelensky called the curb a very dangerous and unpleasant surprise after meeting EU leaders in Brussels, a presidential adviser says talks to lift it are under way, and Trump denies ordering it, while Reuters reported the earlier suspension was decided in the White House rather than at the Pentagon or SpaceX. Moscow is meanwhile fielding Volna Kupol Garant jammers able to degrade service across a 20-square-kilometre grid, and Ukraine has run out of Patriot interceptors, so the margin lost here is not an administrative one. The question that lands in Brussels is who supplies replacement capacity, on what timetable, and whether a European constellation can be contracted before the winter strike campaign settles the matter.

Trump waives the sanctions Congress passed and takes Russian diesel in return

The White House has waived a tranche of Russia sanctions Congress recently legislated, citing national security, and exempted Moscow from the further energy measures the same law mandated. The reprieve follows a deal to release Russian diesel onto world markets, which puts fuel prices rather than Ukraine at the centre of Washington's Russia policy. Zelensky flew to Brussels warning the arrangement is the thin end of a wider rollback, called Moscow's negotiating posture a parody, and asked EU leaders for close to 80 billion dollars more next year; Putin said the security zone Russia plans inside Ukraine is not negotiable. Italian defence minister Guido Crosetto told the BBC the US approach risks prolonging the invasion rather than ending it. For Brussels the arithmetic is simple and unpleasant: every interceptor and every euro Washington withholds now has to be found inside the Union or taken out of the frozen Russian assets nobody has yet agreed to touch.

Brussels confirms a kill switch a week after capping Chinese hybrids

Work is now confirmed on an instrument that would let the Commission shut Chinese goods out of the single market immediately, with leaders asking for a proposal by the end of the year and the bilateral deficit at a record. Beijing's reply was that Europe should not fantasise that a hard line will extract concessions. The understanding reached the previous Friday runs the other way: hybrid and plug-in hybrid imports would be capped at 350,000 vehicles in the first year, potentially from December 2026, rising 6 per cent annually, against 107,000 units in 2024, 302,000 in 2025 and a projected 800,000 this year. European market access in China and rare-earth export licensing sat on the same table, and final EU approval is still outstanding. London has left open the possibility of matching tariffs on Chinese electric vehicles if it joins the Made in Europe package, a step Beijing has already labelled discriminatory.

Paris and Berlin lift cars out of the EU industrial file and write their own rules

A joint Franco-German paper takes car rules out of the Industrial Accelerator Act and moves them into a separate automotive package, with Berlin collecting concessions on combustion engines and Paris limiting which partners get inside Made in Europe. The bargain only became available once the SPD, the junior partner in Merz's coalition, dropped its objection. The same paper sets out how emissions targets are to be weakened, which reopens the combustion-engine phase-out through an industrial instrument rather than a climate one. France then withdrew its opposition to British participation, and Downing Street says it would welcome being included in the Act. Two capitals have pre-negotiated the Union's largest industrial file between them, and the Commission is left deciding whether to adopt their text or defend its own.

Briefing🇨🇳 🇺🇸 🇪🇺 20h ago

Beijing drafts export controls of its own on artificial intelligence and chips

Draft rules published this week would extend Chinese export licensing to advanced artificial-intelligence technology and chips, written to keep the most capable systems inside the country rather than to punish a particular buyer. The symmetry is the part European firms have not priced: after three years of adapting to American controls on what may be sold into China, they now face Chinese controls on what may be bought out of it. Research spending gives the policy its footing, up 8.2 per cent last year to more than 3.9 trillion yuan, about 583 billion dollars, with double-digit growth in fundamental science and university budgets while US research funding is being cut. Washington is tightening on people as well as hardware, with a House committee pressing the Pentagon to blacklist 58 Chinese universities and 62 technology labs. For a European chip designer or model developer the practical change is that a licence decision in Beijing may now sit between it and a component, and the criteria governing those licences are the thing to read when the drafts are finalised.

Beijing's envoy in Manila offers a way out of a quarrel his own side keeps widening

Ambassador Jing Quan told the Second Diliman Dialogue on 15 October, immediately after consultations in Beijing, that relations have reached an extremely sensitive and critical juncture and that maritime disputes have to be handled apart from the rest of the relationship or both sides fall into reciprocal countermeasures. He rejected the premise that Manila must choose between Beijing and Washington, called the relationship no zero-sum game and no contradiction, named Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia and Singapore as states that stay friends with both, and urged a moderate and balanced policy that puts the Philippines first. The conciliatory register sits oddly beside the week's other exchange, in which the two capitals traded accusations over a medical evacuation from the grounded vessel at Second Thomas Shoal. Marcos has been making the economic case alongside the security one, and a China-free manufacturing strategy is a heavy bet for an economy coming off a decade of fast growth. For Brussels, still settling on its own de-risking vocabulary, Manila is the live experiment in whether a middle power can cut Chinese inputs out without losing the growth that pays for its defence.

Yemen's war is closing on the shipping lane meant to be the alternative

Houthi forces have encircled Taiz, one of Yemen's largest cities, in an escalating fight with Riyadh and the factions it backs, after weeks in which they struck Saudi Arabia directly and took ground along the Red Sea coast. South Korea is weighing extra assets for its Cheonghae anti-piracy unit as attacks in the Gulf of Aden climb, which is what a shipping-dependent economy does once routing and insurance stop being somebody else's problem.
