Israel votes and neither bloc reaches 61

Exit polls from Tuesday's election leave Gadi Eisenkot's bloc as the largest group and Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud as the largest single party, with neither side close to the 61 Knesset seats a government needs. German reporting puts the right-religious camp at about 50 seats and the Eisenkot alliance at 52 to 53, on turnout higher than Israel has seen in years. Arab Israeli parties won a record number of seats, which makes them the only arithmetic that resolves the deadlock and the one partnership both camps have ruled out, Netanyahu flatly and Eisenkot explicitly before polling day. Preliminary results were due from midnight and the bargaining that follows can run for weeks, during which Netanyahu stays in office as caretaker and therefore wins the short term whatever the count says. For European capitals the policy gap between the blocs is narrower than the campaign implied: neither would recognise a Palestinian state, and the difference sits in how hard settler violence is policed and how far the courts are protected.

Washington moves hardware into the Gulf and calls it decision time

A third US aircraft carrier is on its way to the Middle East to join two already on station, with an amphibious ready group carrying about 2,500 Marines due mid to late November and another 2,500 Marines already afloat with their own fighter aircraft. Pentagon personnel slated for Bahrain and Kuwait are being sent to CENTCOM (US Central Command) headquarters in Tampa instead, a redirection that tracks preparation rather than deterrence. Trump has put the choice publicly as finishing Iran off or cutting a deal, and what the first round spared is what remains on the target list: energy and civilian infrastructure, which a strike planner would label dual-use. Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani officials met Iran's president on Monday before he flew to Moscow, with a reported billion-dollar offer attached to a Houthi truce, and Brent has slipped below $115 on the prospect of a Riyadh-Tehran understanding that leaves Washington out. US strategic petroleum reserves stand at 282.6 million barrels, which caps how much price relief the administration can manufacture if it does strike, and shipping costs through Hormuz are already elevated by persistent drone attacks.

Berlin shops in Tokyo for raw-materials machinery Brussels does not have

Friedrich Merz lands in Tokyo on Wednesday afternoon local time on the longest foreign trip of his chancellorship, a five-day run through Singapore, Japan and South Korea, with Lars Klingbeil chairing cabinet in Berlin in his place for the fourth time this government. The specific object of interest is JOGMEC, Japan's state raw-materials agency, which puts public money into mining projects worldwide and holds stockpiles, a model German industry has asked Berlin to copy and which a BMW-led initiative will answer in November with its own proposal for a joint raw-materials trading house. Merz used the Singapore leg to call for an EU free trade agreement covering the whole ASEAN bloc, framing it as the next step in trade policy rather than one more bilateral deal. Tokyo also brings defence: whether Germany joins the British-Japanese fighter programme is on the table alongside a German-Japanese business forum and talks with Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi. Brussels is working the same dependency from another angle, saying it wants to invest massively in refining and processing Australian critical minerals as Canberra rewrites its national strategy, which leaves two European raw-materials tracks running without an obvious point of convergence.

A Dandong opening, and guns pointed south

Kim Jong Un's surprise trip to the Chinese border city of Dandong on 24 October included a public inspection of a bus plant, which Seoul analysts read as a push to widen cooperation with Beijing from consumer supply into industrial production, and therefore as a test of where the sanctions regime actually binds. South Korea's foreign minister Cho Hyun told a parliamentary audit he considers it possible that Pyongyang and Beijing discussed restarting North Korea-US talks during the visit, and Dandong residents reported road closures and residential lockdowns that were later scrubbed from social media. Pyongyang opened its 18th autumn international trade fair this week with goods from across Asia and few if any foreign attendees, promoting a domestically branded foldable phone to a room with nobody in it to buy. On the border the movement runs the other way: NK News photographed howitzers at two locations with barrels toward the south and three armoured vehicles visible by Monday, after a disputed landmine blast injured three South Korean soldiers. Switzerland's foreign minister travels to Pyongyang this week to open a political office, the highest-level Western visit in years, while Washington has lifted sanctions on two Chinese animation studios tied to North Korean revenue schemes.

The plenum talks about Party purity while the market falls

The fifth plenum has produced no communique and a steady output of material on Xi Jinping Thought on Party Building, including a front-page People's Daily commentary that invokes the Zhaojin and Yan'an revolutionary traditions and names Xi Zhongxun, the general secretary's father, four times. A Xinhua commentary on 27 October conceded that factors weakening the Party's advanced nature remain widespread and that the anti-corruption fight stays grave and complex, which is the register of a meeting building toward an institutional change rather than one reporting a result. Mainland equities fell again on Tuesday: the Shanghai Composite dropped 1.33 percent to 3,648.30, its lowest close since 12 August 2025, the Shenzhen Component lost 2.50 percent and the STAR 50 ended at 1,252.28, its weakest since March. State-linked funds and brokers have been buying and have not stopped the slide, which matters because the leadership needs equity confidence to channel household savings into technology self-reliance rather than into deposits. Defence Minister Dong Jun is skipping this week's Southeast Asian defence ministers' meeting in Cebu after attending for two years, sending the defence attache from Manila, because the plenum runs across the same days.

A 90-minute line to the Kremlin, opened without the allies

Alexander Stubb and Jonas Gahr Store spent 90 minutes on the phone with Vladimir Putin on Tuesday, the first direct contact between European leaders and the Russian president in more than a year, and Mette Frederiksen said it had not been coordinated with allies, which Helsinki and Oslo both dispute. Putin spent a long stretch of the call on Russian history, and Kyiv has backed the initiative even as other capitals attack it, so the split is among Ukraine's backers rather than between them and Ukraine. Behind the choreography sits a number: the European Commission reckons non-EU partners have pledged about 4 billion euros in aid for Kyiv next year against 19 billion for 2026, which is why Brussels is pressing allies now instead of in the spring. Washington is separately offering postwar commercial terms for US oil majors in Russia as an inducement to bring Putin to the table, and three Russian diesel tankers, two of them under sanctions, are already steaming toward the United States under the October fuel deal. Kyiv has asked its E5 partners for a tenth of their air-defence missile stocks plus money to scale domestic drone production, and a local humanitarian truce took effect in the frontline town of Oleshky to evacuate residents cut off from food and medicine.

Brussels lines up a currency fight after settling the hybrid one

EU economy officials led by director general Maarten Verwey sit down in Beijing in the week of 23 November for a macroeconomic dialogue whose real subject is the renminbi, the first of a run of senior European finance and banking visits over the coming months. The request is that Beijing stop holding its currency down, which is a far harder ask than the hybrid-car quota agreed earlier this month: that deal caps Chinese hybrid exports to the EU at 350,000 units a year against roughly 800,000 expected this year, a figure heading past a million if left alone. European carmakers gain less from the quota than the number suggests, because it pushes Chinese manufacturers toward local production inside Europe and toward higher-margin models rather than out of the market. The EU chamber of commerce chief in China has meanwhile told Europeans to stop fixating on the bilateral trade surplus, which is the argument Beijing would like settled before the currency round opens. Both files answer the same worry in Brussels, that a second China trade shock arrives while European industry is still absorbing the first.

Rehearsals around Taiwan get more specific

US and Japanese forces have spent the second week of the 11-day Keen Sword 27 exercise rehearsing coordinated anti-ship strikes and mobile missile deployments across Kyushu and Japan's southwestern islands, working out how to threaten Chinese warships from several bearings at once while staying hard to target, and practising runway repair under fire. Beijing published high-resolution satellite imagery of Taiwanese landmarks, which defence minister Wellington Koo answered with a promise of continued vigilance against intelligence collection, while the Mainland Affairs Council said Beijing is working to weaken Taipei's ties to Washington. Taiwan has tightened measures against Chinese interference ahead of its 2026 local elections, and polling on the island shows a majority holding an unfavourable view of the Chinese Communist Party. The 2027 US defence bill, covering more than a trillion dollars, returns to a Senate floor after the midterms with China provisions running through most of its sections, and the US has barred Chinese-made BYD vehicles from a base in Japan. European exposure here runs through semiconductors and shipping rather than any treaty commitment, which is why Brussels keeps this file in the trade column while the military one moves faster than the diplomatic one.
