Background on today's names

Person 🇵🇹 🇪🇺

Antonio Costa

Portugal · b. 1961

President of the European Council since December 2024 · Prime Minister of Portugal 2015-2024

A Lisbon lawyer who held interior and justice portfolios, ran the capital as mayor, then led the Socialist Party into eight years in government built on a support arrangement with the parties to its left. He chairs the European Council rather than commanding it: his instruments are the agenda, the bilateral round and the text he puts in front of 27 leaders.

Why it matters. The budget file has been handed back to him personally, with a compromise due before leaders reconvene on 26 November and a deal expected in December. The summit also produced open criticism of his handling, so the next six weeks test whether the chair still has the standing the job needs.

His father, the writer Orlando da Costa, was raised in Goa, and Costa is known there by the Konkani nickname Babush.

Institution 🇪🇺

European Council

The EU's heads of state or government

Sets the Union's general political direction and priorities · no legislative function

Made a formal EU institution by the Lisbon Treaty, it brings together the 27 national leaders with its own president and the Commission president. It works by consensus in practice, and the files it handles are the ones no other body can unlock: the multiannual budget, enlargement, and the Union's response to a crisis.

Why it matters. It is the only venue where the seven-year budget can actually be settled, and it has just failed to settle it for a second time, pushing the decision to a reconvened meeting on 26 November and a December summit.

It has no legislative power at all. Its conclusions bind nobody in law, which is why so much of its output reads as instructions to other institutions rather than as decisions.

The summit argues for two days and hands the budget back to Costa

Antonio Costa leaves Brussels with an instruction rather than an agreement: produce a new compromise on the seven-year budget before leaders reconvene on 26 November, so that a deal can be closed at the December summit. The Irish proposal tabled into this week's European Council drew no support at all, and two days of talks ended with the spending split open and with open unhappiness about how the Council president has handled it. Leaders did adopt conclusions on competitiveness, the next multiannual financial framework and enlargement and reforms on 15 October, which records positions rather than settling them. Energy took the hours the budget needed, running from strategic gas reserves to the future of the Emissions Trading System, and an attempt to soften the emissions rules was held off. Macron and Merz between them hold the votes that would close the gap, and neither has the domestic room to spend them right now, which is what makes December a deadline rather than a forecast.

Brussels drafts a switch that can cut a Chinese firm out of the market

National leaders were told on Thursday that the Commission is open to building a trade instrument carrying a kill switch, one able to eject Chinese companies from the EU market quickly rather than through a case that takes years. The Commission separately showed openness to safeguards on Chinese chemicals and machinery, a brake that covers a whole sector rather than one product line, immediately after the hybrid-car settlement with Beijing. France dropped its objection to the United Kingdom being designated European under the Industrial Accelerator Act, the draft law meant to shield domestic manufacturing from cheap Chinese competition, on condition that London aligns with EU tariffs on Chinese electric vehicles, and UK trade minister Anas Sarwar has indicated it will. Carbon pricing is where the same room divides, because a tighter trade perimeter raises the cost of the climate instruments sitting behind it. Building the weapon before the doctrine is the risk here: a sector-wide brake with no agreed trigger gets reached for politically, and the hybrid-car deal buys respite rather than leverage.

A tanker burns off Romania and the North Atlantic Council takes the file

Two chemical tankers went up in flames off the Romanian coast after Russian drones were spotted in the area, and seven crew were still missing as the search and rescue operation ran into Thursday evening. The North Atlantic Council will now discuss the run of attacks on cargo shipping in the Black Sea, Romanian President Nicușor Dan said, which lifts the question off a national coastguard and onto the alliance. Civilian hulls under third-country flags are the softest target in the theatre and the hardest to answer, because nothing in the sequence obviously reaches an Article 5 threshold while every incident still raises the cost of moving grain and chemicals. Underwriters and routing desks react within days and alliance consultations within months, so the economic damage is taken long before any decision is announced. Romania carries that exposure directly, which currently makes it the cheapest place in the alliance to probe.

Border drones get funded and the watchdogs over them do not

Von der Leyen's letter to the 27 heads of state and government ahead of Thursday's summit reports that screening and the border procedures are operating, and an annex to it obtained by EUobserver sets out how much of the asylum package that entered force in June is still unimplemented. What the letter leaves out is the oversight half of the same law. The gap has a case attached to it: families are still waiting for news of Moroccan migrants who disappeared after a sudden wave of arrivals hit Ceuta, a figure put in the thousands, while the summit agenda ran on fortifying borders. Frontex has suspended its reintegration programme after running through its budget, immediately before a proposed expansion of the agency's role in returns. A returns policy that outgrows its funding and its monitoring at the same time produces litigation faster than it produces removals, and that annex is the document a court reads first.

Seoul recalls an ambassador from Kyiv while the Washington file gets worse

South Korea has recalled its ambassador to Kyiv for consultations over Ukraine's unilateral disclosure that two North Korean prisoners of war were transferred to Seoul, and is now hunting for an exit from a standoff in which Kyiv treats the matter as closed and Seoul still wants a formal apology. In the same week, the relationship with Washington reached its lowest ebb in years, with disputes running from investment commitments through to intelligence leaks. Two senators answered on the deterrence side rather than the trade one: Mark Kelly's Reaffirming Extended Nuclear Deterrence Act, cosponsored by Thom Tillis, would restate the ironclad extended deterrence commitment to Seoul and other treaty allies and tie it to conventional capability and joint exercises. Lee's remarks have taken the argument public, invoking national dignity and declaring that Seoul will not submit, which is defensible at home and expensive in a negotiation Washington still paces; the same day he pressed Evan Greenberg of the US-Korea Business Council to send American capital into Korea, which is the inbound half of a bargain whose outbound half is exactly what is in dispute. Two prisoner transfers should not cost an ambassador, and that they did is the measure of how little slack is left in Seoul's alliance management.

ASML moves on the step that decides whether chips can be stacked

ASML filed 27 bonding patent families between December 2022 and November 2024, most of them die-to-wafer, and The Elec reported in March that architecture design of a hybrid bonder had started with Prodrive Technologies and VDL ETG. The layout carries each die on its own holder along a track past inspection, cleaning, plasma activation and metrology before bonding, instead of a bond head picking dies off a diced wafer, which raises station utilisation and avoids duplicate tooling. Bonding yield is set by alignment accuracy and particle control, both of which are problems ASML already solves in lithography, and that is the whole basis of the claim. Advanced logic adopts first, where die-to-wafer already runs in volume and TSMC's SoIC bonds at about six micrometres of pitch today with 4.5 micrometres targeted around 2029, which puts Besi's logic business at TSMC and Intel directly in the path while the high-bandwidth-memory volume meant to offset that is not expected before 2029, if it arrives at all. TSMC posted a sixth consecutive record quarterly net profit on Thursday on AI hardware demand, so the capital for this build-out is arriving in the same week equity markets began repricing the trade that justifies it.

Berlin's coalition runs on arithmetic that punishes anyone who compromises

A constituency-level analysis circulating in Berlin puts a good third of CDU and CSU deputies outside the Bundestag if a fresh election were held, with SPD losses alongside, which converts every reform vote into a personal survival calculation for the people casting it. Trust inside the coalition fell further after SPD co-chair Bärbel Bas resigned and the accusations that followed, with Handelsblatt's chief reporter describing the relationship as a write-off and doubting the government can regain its footing. Merz told the Financial Times he will drive the unpopular reforms through anyway and cast his party as the firewall, and he opens the Junge Union's Deutschlandtag in Bonn on Friday with the youth wing already unhappy. Industry has stopped waiting for the outcome: a Forsa survey for the restructuring consultancy FTI Andersch finds 15 percent of industrial firms moving production abroad or planning to, rising to 31 percent among firms above 100 million euros of revenue, with 21 percent cutting German capacity. Merz and Macron did settle a car pact in Brussels trading combustion-engine flexibility for tighter Made in Europe rules, but environment minister Carsten Schneider has already put a red line on the climate requirements to Merz in writing, so even that file needs an SPD in no mood to supply votes.

A hundred and thirty million deletions put India's electoral roll on the street

Delhi closed a central park and stepped up security before Friday's rally by the youth-led Cockroach Janta Party, which has demonstrated almost daily for the resignation of the national election authority's chief over the removal of more than 130 million people from the voter lists. An election authority whose own count is the contested object cannot end the argument by finishing the revision, which is what turns an administrative exercise into a constitutional one.

Beijing's ambassador in Manila says the quiet part about Scarborough

Chinese ambassador Jing Quan said Beijing could easily win a conflict in the region if it chose force, and that it chooses peace instead, which Manila read as a thinly veiled threat and answered with calls for diplomatic restraint. The remark landed a day after Philippine defence secretary Gilberto Teodoro called Beijing a regional troublemaker, so this is an escalation in register rather than a slip of phrasing. Pressure at Scarborough Shoal has been building through repeated Chinese moves to drive Philippine aircraft away from the feature, most recently around two weeks ago. Seabed resources are entering the same dispute as rare earth extraction moves offshore, adding a claim type that the existing arbitral framework was never built to handle. A separate unofficial Chinese argument that Batanes belongs to China by way of Taiwan carries no legal weight today, and its usefulness is that it keeps a second pressure point in reserve for the day it is needed.

Naypyidaw buys its way back into ASEAN one bilateral at a time

Min Aung Hlaing met Anwar Ibrahim in Kuala Lumpur on 7 October over the repatriation of Myanmar nationals, the latest stop after Thailand, Laos, Cambodia, Vietnam, China, Russia and India, with Indonesia and Brunei signalled as next. Myanmar Navy vessels began returning about 5,000 asylum seekers from Malaysia in late September, and Rohingya are excluded from those returns because the regime refuses to recognise their citizenship. Ferdinand Marcos Jr, as ASEAN chair, has backed engaging Naypyidaw on the grounds that the Five-Point Consensus has failed, and the regime has now created a committee to run normalisation with the bloc before next month's summit in Manila. Washington is moving the same way, with the assistant secretary of state for intelligence and research, Michael Vance, meeting Min Aung Hlaing on 15 and 16 September to secure the release of Adam Castillo and to press on scam centres, followed by a second quiet meeting in Tokyo inside a month. None of this touches the ground truth: the Arakan Army still holds its sieges of Sittwe and Ramree Island, and the regime answers losses in Rakhine by hitting civilian targets, so recognition is being traded for nothing.

The Gulf risk premium is now rescheduling other people's summits

Missile strikes on Riyadh airport pushed Saudi Arabia to postpone a run of events, and the EU-GCC summit set for 24 October in Riyadh has gone with them, with no new date announced, which removes the main scheduled channel for European Gulf diplomacy this autumn. Washington and Jerusalem both deny preparing a pre-election strike on Iran, while CNN reports planning around the GBU-43, a weapon used once and without the bunker-busting design of the GBU-57 that hit Fordow and Natanz last June, and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps calls an attack imminent. Two ships sank in the Strait of Hormuz, oil is holding high and security in the Red Sea is deteriorating at the same time, so the shipping premium is being paid on both of Europe's southern approaches. The trade map has already redrawn itself around that: China took half of Brazil's oil exports while Gulf supply was disrupted, lifting Brazil-China trade to a record 143 billion dollars over nine months and leaving Brazil a 26.1 billion dollar surplus. Europe's loss here is cheap scheduling, because each postponed summit pushes a file into a bilateral where the bloc negotiates with less weight than it has in a room of 27.
