Why it matters. Schooled in Switzerland alongside her brother, and the first member of the ruling family to set foot in South Korea when she attended the 2018 Pyeongchang opening ceremony.

Ministry of Unification

통일부 · Republic of Korea

Seoul's ministry for inter-Korean relations

Handles contacts, exchanges and the residual machinery of engagement with the North, and drafts the government's formal position on unification. It has survived repeated proposals to abolish it or fold it into the foreign ministry, under administrations of both directions.

Trump offers Putin and Xi a table, and books it in China

Trump told reporters outside the White House on Monday that he would take a three-way meeting with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin during next month's visit to China, with nuclear weapons and denuclearisation as the agenda he wants. Putin had asked for the meeting to settle the Ukraine war on terms favourable to Moscow, so the denuclearisation framing is Trump reclaiming authorship of a summit somebody else proposed; either version seats no Ukrainian and no European. The run-up has been one-directional since the 7 October birthday call: sanctions signed into law last month waived on 16 October before they bit, tariffs on buyers of Russian oil suspended with them, and an energy ceasefire announced that neither belligerent had agreed to, which Kyiv then honoured unilaterally while Russian strikes on its grid continued. Witkoff and Kushner are expected in Norway to meet Zelensky, with a domestic American energy crunch and midterms two weeks out setting the clock. For the Foreign Affairs Council the operative question is no longer how to influence Washington's position but whether European security terms can be negotiated in a format Europe is not invited to, and what the Union does on the morning after one is agreed.

The orbital layer turns out to have an off switch, and Washington holds it

Restrictions placed on Ukraine's Starlink service on the night of 13 October left its drone crews unable to fly, and Reuters traced the order to the White House rather than to SpaceX, which Trump denies issuing personally. Service was partly restored. Russian missiles had reached Kyiv that same night without the usual American advance warning, and the gap has not closed: Ukraine's air force says allies flagged only one of two overnight ballistic attacks this week. Seoul read the lesson immediately. Lee Jae Myung called on Tuesday for a significant expansion of South Korean investment in launch vehicles and satellites, naming Ukraine's loss of US satellite communications and intelligence as the reason, which converts an industrial-policy argument into a defence one. Brussels is circling the same dependency from the commercial end as Europe's four largest telecoms operators confirm a bid for EU satellite spectrum while declining to fund a constellation of their own, leaving the Union with a licensing lever and no asset behind it.

After the weekend, France prices its own disorder

More than half a million people marched on Saturday, 2,400 were arrested, and the interior minister conceded that police lost control in several cities; the mayors of Paris, Lyon and Marseille say entire districts went unprotected and the 19th-century Limoges town hall burned, its clock tower collapsing on film. Curfews followed on Monday, and the French bond sell-off deepened, which is where the political cost becomes a budgetary one for a parliament already unable to pass a budget. Macron said nothing explained the violence and Marine Le Pen called it a profound crisis of trust, a framing contest the Rassemblement National starts ahead of because it needs only the second half to be believed. Looting and clashes the same weekend in Milan, Brussels, Liège and Tirana remove the comfort of treating this as a French pathology. Six months before the presidential election Macron cannot contest, policing decisions now double as decisions about who inherits the argument, and the hard right across the Union gets its talking points from Saturday without having organised anything.

Beijing moves from guarding its market to licensing its know-how

MOFCOM and the science ministry proposed on Friday adding 23 artificial-intelligence technologies, five biomedicine technologies and twelve photovoltaic technologies to the catalogue of items prohibited or restricted for export, with comments open to 15 November but registration duties for covered transfers, including those already under way, in force immediately. The AI list reaches large models, agents, chip design and computing clusters, with public technology excluded, automatic licensing promised for new open releases, and application-programming-interface and software-as-a-service delivery left outside; membership of the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization buys additional facilitation, which is the carrot attached to the catalogue. Thin-wafer, fluidised-bed polysilicon and perovskite tandem production lines would be barred outright while TOPCon, heterojunction and back-contact processes need a licence, and listed equipment carries the technology with it. Finished modules stay exportable, so the instrument targets the ability to rebuild Chinese industrial capacity somewhere else rather than the trade itself. Third-quarter growth of 4.5 per cent, up from 4.3 and above the 4.4 consensus, keeps the annual target in reach and hands the Fifth Plenum a benign backdrop for the self-governance agenda Qiushi has been trailing.

Tokyo counts the months of yttrium it has left while Washington and Beijing draft a deal

Japan is months from exhausting its yttrium supply and has asked Washington to help persuade Beijing to loosen the chokehold before manufacturing lines stop, which puts a shutdown date on a dependency that has so far been discussed as a risk. Negotiators on both sides are meanwhile racing to finalise institutional frameworks and investment packages before the leaders meet in Shenzhen next month, still short of agreement on how to extend the broad trade truce. Monday supplied the counter-pressure: the State Department imposed sanctions under the Protecting American Intellectual Property Act on five Chinese entities including Shanghai Zhisuan Technology, the venture firm Miracle Plus and the convicted former Google engineer Ding Linwei, and the Department of Homeland Security ordered federal civilian agencies to strip out DeepSeek products and anything third parties built on the model. Daiwa and Mizuho collected Chinese securities permits that several American firms are still waiting for, which is Beijing sorting its counterparties by capital rather than by alliance. For European capitals the yttrium case is the usable precedent, since the EU has no equivalent of the bilateral channel Tokyo is reaching for.

A sentence about where South Korea begins turns into an alliance argument

General Xavier Brunson, who commands US Forces Korea and the United Nations Command, said at a Washington think tank last Wednesday that South Korean territory starts at the southern fence of the Demilitarized Zone, a remark initially buried under his caution about transferring wartime operational control. Ruling-party lawmakers led by Kim Byung-joo, Lee Jae Myung's special adviser for defence, raised it on Saturday; by Monday it had reached party leadership meetings and a parliamentary audit where Army Chief of Staff Kim Gyu-ha said he could not agree with it. The defence ministry called it Brunson's personal view and took it to the Pentagon, which local reporting says expressed sympathy with Seoul, while the UNC maintained he was describing armistice duties rather than questioning sovereignty. Democratic Party chair Kim Min-seok then used the row to lock in the existing OPCON transfer framework against the commander's suggested changes, and Lee reaffirmed the 2028 handover target at his 500th-day press appearance. Seoul is also pulling some embassy staff out of Kyiv after the row over disclosing North Korean prisoner-of-war repatriation, so two of its security relationships are being renegotiated at once.

Made in Europe gets written down to what capitals will actually enforce

The Industrial Accelerator Act would tie public spending to European and low-carbon production, and a Jacques Delors Centre brief by Philipp Jäger and Nils Redeker argues the Commission's text would reach only a small share of demand in most sectors while leaving governments broad exemptions to opt out of. Their landing zone keeps the regime open to trade partners where fair competition and supply security allow, tightens anti-circumvention safeguards, and gives local-content and low-carbon requirements enough bite to move manufacturing investment. Member states are pulling the other way, with the Council seeking evidence requirements before Brussels can exclude trading partners from perks meant for local manufacturers. The Franco-German auto pact shows what the trade looks like in practice: Merz got more time for combustion engines, Macron got stricter Made-in-Europe rules, and both now claim to be defending the same industry against Chinese competition while having swapped away the instruments. The Visegrad four want EU money to shield industry from electricity prices, and a European People's Party alliance is pushing to delay the climate levy on fuels, so the industrial agenda is being financed and softened at the same meeting.

The AI build-out starts being priced as credit rather than as growth

Volumes of credit default swaps written on big technology groups are surging as investors buy protection against the build-out unravelling, which moves the AI trade from an equity story to a borrowing one. CoreWeave lost 24 per cent in a week and its bond yields hit new highs, SpaceX's debt rose 74 per cent to $38 billion between December and June, and Meta's long-term debt climbed 42 per cent over the same half-year from a line item that did not exist five years ago. Microsoft walking away from its Nscale deal gave data-centre firms heading for public markets a reference price, and Seoul supplied the live test when the builder Elice fell 32 per cent on its trading debut. The hardware side is still sold out, with Samsung reported to have locked in long-term agreements covering about 80 per cent of planned 2027 memory output, and Taiwanese semiconductor exchange-traded funds hit record investor numbers on 16 October. European exposure runs through the electricity bill rather than the equity: American data centres consumed an estimated 312.6 terawatt-hours in 2025 and US power demand is forecast to grow 3 per cent in 2027 against 0.1 per cent a year from 2005 to 2020, which is the load curve Europe is about to import with its own build-out.
