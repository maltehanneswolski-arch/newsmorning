Person 🇰🇷

Lee Jae Myung

이재명 · b. 1964

President of the Republic of Korea · former Gyeonggi governor and Democratic Party leader

Came to office in 2025 promising pragmatic autonomy: keeping the US alliance while widening cooperation with middle powers, and a 'pacemaker' role between Washington and Pyongyang.

Why it matters. Seoul under him is a candidate partner for European middle-power coalitions, and a government positioning for a nuclear freeze rather than denuclearisation changes what the EU can co-sign.

Worked in factories as a teenager and carries a permanent arm injury from a press machine; qualified for university by examination and practised as a human-rights lawyer.

N

Institution 🇨🇳

NDRC

National Development and Reform Commission · 国家发展和改革委

Macro-planning body under the State Council · successor to the State Planning Commission

Drafts the Five-Year Plans, clears major investment and infrastructure projects, steers industrial policy and still administers some prices.

Why it matters. The capacity and subsidy decisions behind Europe's overcapacity complaints are taken here. MOFCOM defends them in Geneva and Brussels; the NDRC is where they are made.

Known inside the system as the 'little State Council', because so much of the economy has to cross its desk.

A hot inflation print and cold AI sentiment take 7 percent off Seoul

The Korea Composite Stock Price Index closed down 454.59 points, or 7.57 percent, at 5,554.22 on Thursday, after opening 5.59 percent lower and dipping as far as 5,450.81 on its seventh straight decline. The Nikkei 225 fell about 5 percent on the same pairing of rate and AI worries, and the selling began in New York once US consumer prices came in at 3.7 percent for the year to September, up from 3.4 percent in August and driven largely by fuel. Chipmakers led both falls, which lands the damage on exactly the export sector Seoul and Tokyo have built their industrial policy around. At the IMF annual meeting in Bangkok, Kristalina Georgieva put the cost of an AI bust at 1 percent of global output and 1.8 percent of US output, while net borrowing across rich countries heads for 5.2 percent of GDP, the highest since the pandemic. Chief economist Silvana Tenreyro set those figures against a global economy now reaching the limits of its resilience. For European capitals the transmission runs through financing costs rather than equities: the inflation path repricing memory chips is repricing sovereign debt, and the budget summit opening in Brussels has to be funded out of that.

Brussels arms itself for a China shock it has already counted in jobs

Leaders opening the European Council on Thursday are to instruct the Commission to produce new trade instruments against the imbalances they attribute to Chinese industrial policy, wording negotiated into the draft conclusions before the summit began. The trigger was success rather than failure: last week's surprise agreement in Beijing capped Chinese hybrid car imports and secured rare earth supply, and capitals read that as evidence their threats work. Berlin brings the arithmetic that carries the argument domestically, with the German trade deficit with China set to pass 100 billion euros this year, roughly 90 billion more than in 2019, imports 62 percent above the 2019 level and exports about 25 percent below it. The employer-funded IW institute attributes the gap to subsidies and an undervalued currency, while the BDI industry federation counts up to 15,000 German jobs lost a month, concentrated in cars, electrical equipment and machine building. A second-strike instrument against economic coercion sits on the same day's agenda, which would hand the Union a retaliation tool it has so far only described.

Kyiv loses its drones' top speed and its early warning in the same week

A speed ceiling imposed overnight on Ukraine's Pentagon-funded Starlink terminals severs the link to any drone moving faster than about 100 miles an hour, grounding much of the medium-range fleet and removing one of the few means Kyiv had to intercept Russian attack drones. Zelensky said Ukraine also received no advance warning of the Russian ballistic missiles that struck Kyiv on two consecutive nights, with more than 600,000 people left without power. Musk replied that any limits are decided by the US military and ultimately the president, while Poland, which pays for Ukraine's terminals, accused him of playing politics with his products. Zelensky has now told the Financial Times that Ukraine will resume strikes on Russian refineries, ending the restraint he had held since Trump declared an energy truce that Moscow never observed. Trump's negotiators separately floated unfreezing some Russian assets to restart talks, a proposal European officials balked at in Miami.

The money fight the summit cannot finish and Ukraine cannot wait for

Leaders argued the next multiannual budget face to face for the first time on Thursday, and the working assumption in Brussels is now that December arrives without agreement. Macron and Merz hold the keys between them, and both are constrained enough at home that neither can spend political capital on a deal. Marine Le Pen came to Brussels to campaign for a smaller Union and a cut in the French contribution, opening a second front behind the French position. Underneath the argument sits Ukraine, where a financing gap of up to 70 billion euros opens next year, while the draft declaration promises full and determined support along with a new initiative to supply Kyiv with satellite data. Every emergency loan so far has been presented as a one-off, and the question leaders keep deferring is whether long-term joint borrowing has quietly become the Union's standing method.

Europe traded one gas dependence for another and the bill arrived early

The United States supplied 57 percent of EU liquefied natural gas last year and is on course for two thirds this year, which converts a commercial relationship into the same class of exposure Europe spent three years escaping. Energy prices are high enough to push the budget talks off the first day of the summit agenda, with Brent back above 106 dollars after a ballistic missile hit a Saudi Aramco operations centre. Hong Kong bus operators have already applied for fare rises citing Middle East war costs, the kind of second-order pass-through that reaches household politics quickly. The Black Sea has become a second front in the grain war after drone strikes on two ships in Bulgarian waters, and no alternative EU route exists to move those cargoes out. Energy commissioner Dan Jorgensen's case for diversification collides with the arithmetic that the diversification has run almost entirely in one direction.

A bridge twelve years late opens while the submarines keep firing

The New Yalu River Bridge opened to cargo at 10am local time on Thursday, more than a decade after Beijing finished building it, with Xinhua footage showing trucks crossing. Pyongyang tested two types of submarine-launched strategic cruise missile in the East Sea on Wednesday, days after unveiling a weapons museum and firing ballistic missiles, and KCNA said the launches threatened no neighbour. US prosecutors charged fourteen people over schemes placing North Korean IT workers inside American firms, and the FBI seized 187 devices from laptop farms. Houthi forces have taken Red Sea islands and struck Saudi Arabia's main airport with weapons assembled in Yemen from North Korean designs, giving Pyongyang both revenue and a proving ground it lacked two years ago. The bridge moves the sanctions question from interdiction to volume, because a road crossing built for trucks is harder to monitor than rail and easier to scale.

Delhi holds 273 companies of paramilitary police in the capital

Delhi Police have asked to keep 273 companies of Central Armed Police Forces in the capital until 20 October, out of 301 approved across two tranches on 6 and 8 October. For scale, Manipur had 218 companies available in November 2024 during an active ethnic conflict, in a state roughly fifteen times the size of Delhi. Four police cases have been filed over social media posts about the protests against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar, more than 500 posts were taken down by Tuesday evening, and Jantar Mantar has been denotified as a protest site. The pressure is consolidating the opposition rather than splitting it, with Rahul Gandhi and INDIA bloc leaders condemning the arrests and a tricolour protest called for Connaught Place on 16 October. Running against all of that, India approved the largest foreign takeover of a domestic bank and Hyundai mapped out 15.5 billion dollars of Indian spending on plants and AI, so the investment case and the civil liberties case are moving in opposite directions.

The Gulf war premium reaches Hong Kong bus fares and Japanese travel advice

A ballistic missile hit a Saudi Aramco operations centre late Tuesday after Saudi-backed forces struck a Houthi oil depot in Yemen, and Brent sits above 106 dollars even though Riyadh added two million barrels a day of output last month. Houthi forces have captured Red Sea islands and hit Saudi Arabia's main airport, and a Singapore-flagged container ship sank in the Strait of Hormuz after being struck. Japan raised its travel alert for parts of Saudi Arabia and the Asian Football Confederation is weighing postponement of the Asian Cup, which is how a security shock starts appearing in ordinary calendars. Fortifications have gone up around Iran's sites at Pickaxe Mountain and Natanz, while reporting points to a large-scale operation discussed with Israel ahead of the US midterms. Repeated penetration of US-supplied air defences is the detail Gulf buyers will price, since more interceptors cannot repair a system that is being read accurately.
