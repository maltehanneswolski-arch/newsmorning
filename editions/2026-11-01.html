Know the players

Background on today's names

Person

Karol Nawrocki

Poland · b. 1983

President of Poland 2025- · President of the Institute of National Remembrance 2021-25 · Director, Museum of the Second World War 2017-21

A Gdansk historian who ran the state institute that writes and litigates Poland's account of the communist and wartime past, and who reached the presidency in 2025 as an independent backed by Law and Justice after a second round decided by roughly two points against Rafal Trzaskowski.

Why it matters. He will not administer the oath to the constitutional court judges the Sejm has elected, which is the act that has left the tribunal unable to seat them and turned an appointments dispute into a confrontation with the Tusk government.

He boxed as an amateur in Gdansk clubs and still trains, and he published historical work under a pen name while running the remembrance institute.

Institution

Sejm

Sejm Rzeczypospolitej Polskiej · Warsaw

Lower house of the Polish parliament · 460 deputies on four-year terms · elects the judges of the Constitutional Tribunal

Elected by proportional representation with a five percent threshold for parties and eight percent for coalitions. It passes statute, can override the Senate by an absolute majority, and chooses the fifteen judges of the constitutional court for single nine-year terms.

Why it matters. Its judge elections are the object of the fight in Warsaw: the chamber has chosen new judges, the president will not administer their oath, and without that oath they cannot take their seats.

Its 1505 Nihil novi constitution barred the king from issuing law without the chamber's consent, four generations before the English Bill of Rights.

A third weekend on the streets, and a new word for it

More than 320,000 people marched across France on Saturday in a third consecutive weekend against living costs and the state of public services, and the day closed with tear gas in Paris and Marseille after what interior minister Laurent Nunez called an extraordinary level of violence from a minority at the end of about 150 otherwise peaceful rallies. Germany ran its own version the same day: a collective calling itself Gen Z regelt! put marches into 34 cities and demanded more money for education, and shops were looted in Berlin, Hamburg and Frankfurt. Portugal joined too, and the political scientist Donatella della Porta argues the French footage is what carries the movement across borders, alongside a shift from crowd control toward counterinsurgency policing. The vocabulary is what changed in the last few weeks: demonstrators in Italy folded Ukraine into a general demand for peace and less military spending, and Alice Weidel, Marine Le Pen and Bulgaria's prime minister have each reached for the same formula. Anne Applebaum's objection is that nobody using it has costed the alternative, since a European funding stop would break the Ukrainian line rather than quiet it, and a collapsed front produces refugee flows and a rearmed Russian army rather than a smaller defence budget.

Warsaw: the oath nobody will administer

Karol Nawrocki will not swear in the constitutional court judges the Sejm has elected, and without that oath the tribunal cannot seat them, which is how an appointments quarrel became a court that cannot sit. On Friday the government's side suspended the tribunal's president, protesters breached the perimeter fence and several were detained, and riot police were posted around the building. Nawrocki walked through the crowd into the court around midnight and spent Saturday in crisis talks. Tusk took a prime-time slot on Saturday night to say the overhaul will proceed, presenting it as repair of an institution that years of disputed appointments had already crippled. The Commission has asked Nawrocki to administer the oath to parliament's nominees, which is close to the limit of what Brussels can reach: no treaty instrument addresses a head of state declining a ceremonial act, and with legislative elections a year away both sides gain more from keeping the dispute open than from settling it.

Iran waits out the American calendar

Trump said the United States still has one or two targets left in Iran and could take them in ten minutes, adding that there is no hurry because Iranian military and nuclear capacity has already been wrecked; he has separately pointed at the window after Tuesday's midterms. Rubio says no decision has been made. Mojtaba Khamenei told his commanders that any level of threat must draw an immediate response with no geographical limitation, a formula that covers Gulf shipping and American bases rather than Israel alone. The maritime file is where this reaches Europe: Tehran has restricted traffic through Hormuz since the war opened on 28 February, Oman lifted 25 people off a burning ship this week, and Iran's top diplomat says a plan agreed with Qatar would reopen the strait within two days if Washington meets Tehran's conditions. Araghchi confirmed a letter from Vance while denying any plan for talks, and Pakistan's chief mediator has been to Tehran twice this month alongside Saudi and Turkish officials, so the channels exist even though the parties say they are not using them.

Patriots on paper, grain the long way round

Washington has approved a possible sale of 500 Patriot interceptors to Ukraine and Zelensky's answer was that deliveries could take years, which is the distance between an export licence and a round in a launcher. The Black Sea is the other half of the problem: Bulgaria is asking the EU for a further two billion euros as attacks on shipping mount, MEPs are pressing for that money, and Russian grain is now leaving through Arctic ports thousands of miles from the farms, which puts the cost on buyers who have nothing to do with the war. Crews carry that exposure personally, and the war-risk premium prices it without reducing it. Around Oleshky the two sides held a local ceasefire long enough to pull several hundred starved residents out, some of whom stayed behind, and the Russian plan to film the operation for propaganda was undercut by the pictures of the arrivals. European money is now effectively the whole of the supply, which is what turns a halt-the-aid plank in the Bulgarian presidential campaign into a procurement question rather than a rhetorical one.

Made in Europe lands badly in East Asia

Merz says the Franco-German plan to restrict car subsidies has caused considerable concern in East Asian capitals, and that the reaction surprised him. The surprise is the part worth examining: Korean and Japanese carmakers assemble inside the EU, so a subsidy conditioned on European content reads in Seoul and Tokyo as a rule aimed at Chinese imports that will catch allied plants on the wrong side of a threshold. Both countries hold trade agreements with the EU whose national-treatment provisions are the obvious place to contest it, and both are being courted at the same time on chips, batteries and security cooperation. The commercial pressure behind the reaction is real, with Asian manufacturers already defending share against Chinese electric rivals and one Toyota-backed truckmaker betting on its aftersales network to hold ground against them. Whatever the plan does for European assembly lines, it is being read abroad as a move toward the local-content conditionality that Brussels objected to when Washington wrote it into the Inflation Reduction Act.

Pyongyang tests a cluster warhead and banks six billion dollars

The Missile Administration tested a cluster-bomb warhead for tactical ballistic missiles on Saturday and set out plans for units to use four categories of them by the end of the year, with state media claiming a single round can burn out 12 to 13 hectares. Against dispersed artillery or an airfield apron, that changes the arithmetic of a short-range salvo far more than another range record would. The FBI then attributed the theft of more than six billion dollars from Binance on 29 October to Trader Traitor, the group it associates with Pyongyang, making it the largest single crypto theft on record; Binance says it has covered about 90 percent of the loss and is restoring withdrawals. Sanctions enforcement is the part that fails here, because a programme funding itself through exchange compromises sits outside measures designed for trade and banking. Seoul used the G20 to restate its position on the 21 September mine blast in the DMZ that wounded three soldiers and to press for military talks, which Pyongyang has answered by denying responsibility and promising action along the border.

A Politburo seat for the man who ran the purge

The Party has given a Politburo seat to the general who ran the army's internal police through most of Xi Jinping's clear-out of his own high command, which places the instrument of the purge inside the body that supervises it. Analysts quoted in Taipei read a decimated command as lowering near-term war risk, on the argument that an officer corps under investigation does not want a campaign. That cuts against the 2027 framing hardening in Washington, where the push for Indo-Pacific combat readiness in that year has turned a date US officers once attached to a possible move on Taiwan into a planning benchmark, even though the March intelligence assessment said Beijing has no plan to attack in 2027 and no fixed timeline for unification. The daily pressure is maritime: Taiwan's coast guard says Chinese vessels are calling ships on the east coast by radio and asserting a jurisdiction that does not exist, on the one approach that matters if the western ports are closed. A military under political reconstruction is less predictable in the grey zone rather than less active in it, and the coast guard is the arm that shows it first.

Atlanta closes without a communique

The G20 foreign ministers finished in Atlanta with a chairman's statement instead of a joint communique, divisions over Russia's war being the reason no common text was reachable. Rubio's own agenda was supply chains: he claimed universal recognition among members on critical minerals, supply-chain security and freedom of navigation, which is the language Washington uses for loosening China's hold on processing and refining. Kallas warned that restoring Putin to the G20 table would normalise Russian war crimes, and rebuffed reported Pentagon requests that allies soften their criticism of Moscow. Seoul worked the margins, with foreign minister Cho Hyun meeting Kallas on cyber and space cooperation and holding bilaterals with Britain, India and Mexico. A chairman's statement lets a chair record an agenda without owning a disagreement, so the signal to read is which files the Americans wanted on the page rather than what the twenty settled.

Why ByteDance denies that it won

A Nomura note built on Quest Mobile data put ByteDance apps at 40.9 percent of time spent on China's fifty most-used mobile apps in July against Tencent's 29.1 percent, with the micro-drama app Hongguo alone drawing 168 million daily users, more than the four leading streaming services combined. The commentator Qin Shuo asked whether the Chinese internet had passed from Tencent's social era into ByteDance's algorithmic one, and the company's communications executive Li Liang called the label complete nonsense, attacked the data as narrow, and pointed at Qin's appearance at a Tencent creators' event and his earlier work on books authorised by the rival. A firm does not fight that hard to disown dominance unless dominance is expensive: where anti-monopoly cases are settled in public narrative before any file is opened, an accepted story about market share is what regulators act on. The second charge is industrial rather than legal, and it is the one Beijing minds. A viral comparison claiming the United States spends its compute on code and enterprise research while China spends its own on revenge-fantasy micro-dramas drew a People's Daily rebuttal, which is how a line about gunpowder and fireworks turned into an argument about what a national champion is for.

Delhi polices a march about the voter roll

Security forces carrying batons and shields, with riot control vehicles behind them, ringed the site of an opposition rally in Delhi on Sunday called to demand the resignation of India's election chief over a revision of the voter lists. Congress is leading it under Rahul Gandhi, organisers expect hundreds of thousands, and the parties staged a mock impeachment of the poll chief the day before. The objection is arithmetic: critics put the number of voters who could be struck off at 130 million and argue the revision favours Modi's party. The march also joins two strands of agitation that had largely run separately, the party opposition and the youth-led Cockroach Janta Party, which makes it a harder problem than the sequence of rallies India has seen since the summer. For anyone treating India as a partner rather than a market, the contested object here is the referee, and arguments about electoral administration outlast the election they are about.

Tuesday in the United States, and what it sets

Americans vote on Tuesday and several files that matter in Brussels are waiting on the result. Trump has tied further strikes on Iran to the period after the vote, so Gulf shipping risk now has a date attached to it. Bond managers have stopped taking positions until the result and the economic releases land, while Steve Bannon is predicting open Republican conflict and heavy legislative fights before the new Congress is sworn in. The campaign is being fought through a broken contact layer: Democrats made 300 million phone attempts in 2024 and got through 3 percent of the time, and 56 percent of Americans now say they have stopped discussing political news with someone over something that person said, against 45 percent in 2024. Arizona is the state to watch for whether the Latino shift back toward the Democrats holds, with purchasing power and immigration enforcement both doing work there.
