Know the players

Background on today's names

KJ

Person 🇰🇵

Kim Jong Un

North Korea · in power since 2011

General Secretary of the Workers' Party of Korea · President of the State Affairs Commission · Supreme Commander of the armed forces

Inherited the state in December 2011 and spent the following decade collecting titles that were created or renamed to fit him, ending the collective-leadership fiction his father had maintained. Since 2024 the relationship with Moscow has supplied money, fuel and combat experience without requiring any of the concessions Beijing used to attach.

Why it matters. His first recorded crossing of the New Yalu River Bridge puts China back into a competition it had largely conceded to Russia. Which side he leaves Dandong aligned with determines how much of the sanctions architecture still has a physical chokepoint behind it.

He travelled to the 2019 Hanoi summit by armoured train through China, roughly sixty hours in each direction, rather than fly.

🇨🇳

Institution 🇨🇳

Central Foreign Affairs Commission

中央外事工作委员会 · CCP body

Party organ that sets foreign policy · chaired by Xi Jinping · staffed by its Office

Upgraded from a leading small group to a commission in the 2018 reorganisation, part of a wider shift of decision-making from state ministries to Party bodies. The foreign ministry executes what is decided here.

Why it matters. A Kim visit is handled by the Party rather than by the MFA, so the readout that matters will come from this side of the system. With the fifth plenum sitting from Monday, the same body is setting the external line for the next five-year frame.

Its existence means China has two foreign-policy addresses, and only one of them appears in most European contact lists.

Kim Jong Un crosses into Dandong on a bridge he had never used

Kim Jong Un entered the Chinese border city of Dandong on Saturday over the newly opened New Yalu River Bridge, unannounced by Pyongyang and confirmed by Seoul's unification ministry through Yonhap at around 3 p.m. Korean time. The bridge sat unused for twelve years, derided locally as a road to a vegetable patch, until trucks began crossing it; the leader using it himself converts a trade artery into a political one. A Chinese veterans affairs delegation led by vice minister Hong Zongming had already arrived in North Korea on Thursday for the 76th anniversary of China's entry into the Korean War, which looks like groundwork rather than coincidence. Since 2024 Pyongyang has taken most of what it needs from Moscow, so a visit to Beijing is a bid to reopen an auction that had only one bidder. For European sanctions policy the consequence is concrete: two patrons competing for the same client means enforcement gaps widen on both the Chinese and the Russian side of the border at once.

Seoul empties its Kyiv embassy over two disclosed prisoners

South Korea has pulled embassy staff out of Kyiv on top of recalling its ambassador, over Volodymyr Zelensky's disclosure at the UN General Assembly that Ukraine had handed two North Korean prisoners of war to Seoul. The Lee Jae Myung government calls that a breach of a confidentiality agreement and has refused to accept an apology as sufficient. The stated grievance is the safety of the prisoners' families in the North; the working grievance is that publicity cuts across Lee's attempt to open talks with Pyongyang, an attempt from which he has already removed denuclearization as a precondition. Pyongyang has said nothing at all about the transfer, even while Kim Yo Jong issues threats over loudspeakers and warning shots, and that silence is plausibly what Seoul was trying to protect. Since late 2024 the North has sent as many as 25,000 soldiers and between 12,000 and 25,000 labourers to Russia, and Ukrainian disclosure has been the main reason any of it is publicly documented, which is precisely why Kyiv is unlikely to stop. (CSIS Korea Chair)

Starlink stays throttled over Ukraine and Europe starts buying alternatives

Ukrainian forces report continuing connection interruptions on Starlink days after Donald Trump said Elon Musk had removed the speed limits, and Kyiv has accused Washington of deliberately degrading a link its crews use to intercept incoming Russian attacks. Some drone pilots describe improvement, which makes the problem harder to contest than an outage would be: a service can be slowed without ever being switched off, and no contract language covers that. Kyiv has answered by signing a satellite agreement with Finland and a letter of intent with ICEYE and Nokia, a hedge rather than a substitute given the constellation sizes involved. In the same week Musk accused unnamed oligarchs of obstructing Starlink in India, a market where American reliability was already being re-examined. The procurement question for European defence ministries is no longer whether a supplier is allied but whether the service degrades quietly when politics shift, and that is not something a tender document currently scores.

Delhi concedes a standoff on the eastern border it had been denying

Indo-Tibetan Border Police director-general Shatrujeet Kapur told an ITBP event that Indian and Chinese troops have confronted each other at several points in Arunachal Pradesh over recent days, which is the first official Indian acknowledgement of how far tensions on the eastern Line of Actual Control have gone. A 281 gigabyte AfD leak lands while Berlin pitches for foreign capital

Close to half a million emails and documents from the AfD, 281 gigabytes in all, are now in the open, reaching back to the party's founding years and including correspondence from Alice Weidel and Alexander Gauland. The material is assessed as authentic; the party has confirmed the theft, called it an attack on the German party system and on democracy, and said nothing about what is in it. Several newsrooms are already working through the archive, so the damage will arrive in instalments over weeks rather than in one story, and the security failure it exposes is as consequential as any individual mail. The same week the federal government staged its first Invest in Germany summit, in Erich Honecker's former office, collecting billion-euro commitments from Nvidia and Amazon while investors repeated the same list of deterrents. With the SPD searching for a new leadership and Markus Soeder counting his loyalists, the coalition's attention is on its own arithmetic at the moment a hostile archive starts being read in public.

The EU and Canada meet next week with the label still unagreed

EU and Canadian leaders meet in Canada on 29 and 30 October, with a Brussels press briefing on the 27th, and the associate membership formula Ursula von der Leyen floated in her 2026 State of the Union address still has no agreed meaning. Germany is leading the governments resisting the term, and the objection is about precedent rather than about Canada: a defined category between membership and partnership would be read the same day in Ankara, London and the Western Balkans capitals. The substance under discussion is less contested than the name on it, covering defence industrial access, participation in procurement instruments and raw materials cooperation, all of which can be delivered through existing agreements. Leaving the label undefined keeps the summit comfortable and transfers the argument to the enlargement file, where an undefined intermediate status is worth more to those who want to slow accession than to those who want to reward it. A crowded November follows, with the EU-Western Balkans summit on 25 November and a Special European Council on 26 and 27 November in the Europa building.

Brussels reaches for safeguards as Beijing writes down a Turkish deposit

Maros Sefcovic said the EU may put quotas or tariffs on further Chinese industries before opening another round of negotiations, naming plastics as a sector where investigations are well advanced, and pointed at safeguards as the instrument when speed matters more than talks. Safeguards respond to import surges and do not require proving dumping or subsidy, which is what makes them fast and also what makes retaliation easier to justify on the other side. On the inputs side of the same relationship, Chinese researchers have published a detailed geological study of Kizilcaoren, Turkey's largest known rare earth deposit. Their conclusion matters more to Brussels than to Ankara, because the nearest substantial non-Chinese deposit on the Union's doorstep is being discounted by scientists working for the incumbent supplier. Any European raw materials plan that penciled Kizilcaoren in as part of the answer now needs a different number, and the gap has to be filled by processing capacity or by recycling rather than by a new mine. Two ways around Nvidia, and only one country can try both

Amazon has rebuilt Trainium's software stack around native PyTorch instead of bolting it on through PyTorch/XLA, hired the engineer who led Google's native Torch-on-TPU effort, and runs roughly 80,000 physical cores on automated quality control for the result. Anthropic already serves production inference and reinforcement-learning rollouts on Trainium2 and Trainium3, so the contested ground is research training, where CUDA maturity still decides most purchases. China cannot run the equivalent experiment: advanced-node capacity at SMIC is estimated at 60,000 wafers a month, Huawei has first call on it for both Ascend accelerators and Kirin phone silicon, and a new design house cannot secure a slot after tapeout however good its chip is. That is why the wave of HBM-free inference startups in the United States has no Chinese counterpart, even though Chinese venture funding for chip design has outrun American funding every year since at least 2016. For European buyers the pricing consequence is immediate, and SK hynix has just ended Solidigm's 150 billion dollar listing review 79 days after floating it rather than sell into a semiconductor correction.
