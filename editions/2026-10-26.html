Person 🇷🇸

Aleksandar Vucic

Serbia · president since 2017

President of Serbia · founder and long-time leader of the Serbian Progressive Party · prime minister 2014 to 2017

Built the Serbian Progressive Party into a machine that held parliament, the presidency and most of the media space for fourteen years, running an accession track with Brussels and a political relationship with Moscow at the same time and refusing to choose between them. That balance was the product, and it is what Sunday's result has just priced.

Why it matters. Conceding a parliamentary election he was expected to manage removes the single point of control in Serbian politics and puts the frozen accession chapters back into play before the EU-Western Balkans summit on 25 November.

He denounced the student movement as a foreign-run operation to unseat him and was endorsed by Vladimir Putin, and still conceded within hours of the exit polls rather than contesting them.

Institution 🇷🇸

Narodna skupstina

Serbia · national assembly

Unicameral 250-seat parliament · elects and dismisses the government · proportional representation from a single nationwide constituency

Serbia elects all 250 members from one country-wide list under proportional representation, which is why national swings translate almost directly into seats and why a movement with no local machine can convert protest into a majority in a single cycle. The chamber appoints the government, so the list that holds it chooses the prime minister.

Why it matters. The 135 to 140 seats attributed to the Student List in exit polls is the number that matters, because 126 is a majority of the chamber and anything above it means a government can be formed without the outgoing party.

Electing an entire parliament from a single nationwide constituency is unusual in Europe and removes local incumbency almost entirely, which cuts both ways for whoever is in power.

Vucic concedes and the Serbian accession file reopens

Aleksandar Vucic acknowledged defeat shortly after 9.30pm on Sunday, exit polls putting his Serbian Progressive Party on 105 seats against 135 to 140 for the reformist Student List led by cardiologist Ilija Srdanovic, ending a 14-year hold on power. The list grew out of nearly two years of demonstrations set off by the Novi Sad railway canopy collapse that killed 16 people in 2024, and it campaigned without a conventional party leadership. Vucic had Vladimir Putin's endorsement and had cast the students as a foreign-run attempt to unseat him, so the scale of the loss is a verdict on that argument as much as on his record. Serbia's accession chapters have been frozen for years and a government carrying no obligation to Moscow is the first plausible unlock, with the EU-Western Balkans summit on 25 November the first scheduled place to test it. Election day brought reports of violence and allegations of irregularities, which is the thread to watch if the losing side contests seat allocation rather than the headline result.

The fifth plenum opens on discipline rather than on growth

The fifth plenum of the 20th Central Committee opened in Beijing on Monday for four days, carrying a Politburo work report and a state-media line that adherence to Xi Jinping's leadership is what makes the rest work. Disciplinary authorities opened investigations into more than a million people last year, over a hundred of them at or above ministerial or provincial rank, a pace that has not slowed since the campaign began in 2012. Anhui, Henan, Shanxi and Xinjiang closed their party congresses and seated new standing committees the week before, the provincial half of a five-year cycle that terminates at next year's national congress. Xi is widely expected to take a fourth term there and run the reshuffle that goes with it, so a plenum spent on loyalty rather than on the economy says more about succession management than about policy. The operational read for European capitals is that counterparts in Chinese ministries are working under audit pressure, which slows decisions and raises the rank at which anything unusual has to be signed.

European rearmament runs into its own yards and assembly lines

Airbus will open talks with BAE Systems about a role in GCAP, the sixth-generation fighter programme run by the UK, Italy and Japan, and Berlin is weighing whether to join after its rival FCAS partnership with France and Spain collapsed. That would put Germany in a combat-air programme with Tokyo instead of Paris, a realignment that reaches every European aerospace supply chain and undercuts the argument that defence integration necessarily runs through the Franco-German pair. At the other end of the scale the Bundeswehr is preparing to buy the Ukrainian Bars strike system, the first offensive weapon developed in Ukraine that it has purchased, which treats Ukrainian industry as a supplier rather than only as a recipient. Naval capacity is the binding constraint: member states are struggling to reinforce the Aspides mission in the Red Sea because the hulls and the shipyard slots do not exist. The limit on ambition has moved from budget lines to industrial throughput, and no decision taken this year adds a yard before the end of the decade.

Brussels edges from blocking the buyer to being the buyer

Lawmakers want the Commission to take direct equity stakes in raw-material projects, copying instruments the United States and Japan already run rather than relying on tariffs and investment screening. Equity moves the institutional question from whether Europe can block a Chinese acquirer to whether it can be the acquirer, which needs a balance sheet, a mandate and a tolerance for commercial loss the EU budget does not currently carry. The fiscal backdrop argues against it: debt-servicing costs, a payout overhang and fraud exposure have been described as a pressure cooker, with repayment of past joint borrowing still unsettled. The counter-argument is scale, since transatlantic trade ran at roughly two trillion dollars in 2025 and the technology race is the stated reason Washington needs Europe at all. The realistic landing zone is a pilot attached to named projects rather than a general facility, because that is the only version the next budget can absorb.

Chinese industry stops exporting to Europe and starts living there

BYD, Chery and Geely are turning Europe from an export destination into a manufacturing base, taking over underused plants and pairing with incumbents to get inside the tariff wall rather than argue with it. Ming Yang is running the same play in wind power, offering a joint venture with Octopus and separate legal entities meant to satisfy European buyers that turbine data cannot reach Beijing, which substitutes corporate structure for trust the hardware cannot establish on its own. Huawei, barred from lobbying the institutions, has moved money into sponsoring the Brussels events circuit, reaching the same officials through a channel the ban does not name. Each of these answers a European instrument structurally: tariffs, lobbying bans and screening all police a border, while a factory, a joint venture or a conference sponsorship sits on the inside of it. The question for the next Commission cycle is whether ownership and data rules reach production located within the single market, because that is where the exposure now sits.

Trump opened the week telling reporters that Russia has been fine on Ukraine, the warmest line on Moscow he has taken and one that lands while his administration looks increasingly willing to put the Ukrainian file down. From India to Canada to the Gulf and Europe, governments are building workarounds to an American-led order they no longer treat as fixed, and the sharpest consequence is the number of states under the US nuclear umbrella now examining deterrents of their own. A Europe-Canada track that firms up while the US-Canada deal is still open gives Ottawa leverage in both rooms and gives Brussels a partner with the critical minerals and the Arctic position it has been shopping for. The line to read in the Montreal text is defence industrial cooperation, which is where a communique either turns into procurement or does not.
