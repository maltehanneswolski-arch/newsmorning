Benjamin Netanyahu

Israel · prime minister

Prime Minister of Israel · leader of Likud · longest-serving holder of the office

Returned to office at the end of 2022 at the head of a coalition whose religious and far-right components each hold an effective veto over the whole, which is why settlement and West Bank policy has been the most consistent part of Israeli governance through an otherwise unstable period. Tuesday is the first national vote since the 7 October 2023 attack, and the campaign has been fought on security and on the condition of Israeli democracy rather than on the economy.

Why it matters. With both blocs polling short of 61 seats he remains the pivot of the coalition arithmetic even in defeat, and the EU's handling of Israel over the coming months depends on which side of that arithmetic he ends up on.

He spent the eve of the vote advertising Donald Trump's endorsement, saying it is doubtful Israel will ever again have such a friendly American president.

Institution 🇮🇱

Knesset

Israel · unicameral parliament

Israel's legislature · 120 seats · nationwide proportional representation with a 3.25 percent threshold

Every seat is allocated from a single national constituency, so the parties that scrape over the threshold decide who governs. A coalition needs 61 of the 120 seats and no single party has ever won that alone, which is why Israeli elections are followed by negotiation rather than by a handover.

Why it matters. Both leading blocs are polling below 61, which moves the outcome into bargaining inside the chamber on a timetable of weeks rather than days, and that is the period in which European capitals have to decide whom they are dealing with.

The electoral threshold was raised from 2 percent to 3.25 percent in 2014, and a list that finishes below it wins no seats at all.

Israel votes with neither bloc near 61 seats

Polling stations opened across Israel on Tuesday in the first national vote since the 7 October 2023 attack, and the published surveys leave neither Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc nor the opposition close to the 61 Knesset seats a government needs. That arithmetic points to weeks of coalition bargaining rather than a result on the night, which is the part European capitals are least prepared for. Netanyahu spent the eve of the vote advertising Donald Trump's endorsement and suggesting the two could restart the war on Iran if he wins, tying the Israeli result to the same escalation file that brought Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani officials to Tehran on Monday. Berlin is watching without speaking: the chancellery and the foreign ministry are both holding their tongues while Armin Laschet, who chairs the Bundestag foreign affairs committee, has said publicly that Netanyahu's challenger Gadi Eisenkot would better represent a fresh start for Israeli politics. What the ballot cannot reach is the West Bank, where the UN Independent International Commission of Inquiry found on Monday that Israeli forces have committed the crime against humanity of extermination, and where the machinery runs through ministries and budgets that outlast coalitions.

Pyongyang detours through Dandong while the line stays unagreed

Kim Jong Un's unannounced one-day trip to the Chinese border city of Dandong last week was commercial in shape rather than ceremonial: he toured the Huanghai Automobile plant, which has previously built vehicles rebranded for the North Korean market, alongside a long-delayed border bridge. South Korean Foreign Minister Cho Hyun told a National Assembly audit on Tuesday that the two sides may have touched on reviving a Washington channel, while warning against reading a one-day trip that far. Foreign Minister Choe Son Hui travelled with him carrying a handbag valued above $3,500, which is worth noting only because luxury goods are exactly what the sanctions regime is written to stop. The military track runs the other way: Pyongyang wrote to the president of the UN Security Council accusing Seoul of staging the DMZ landmine blast that injured ROK soldiers, and Seoul's offer of military talks on the Military Demarcation Line meets the problem that fixing the line obliges one side to give ground after seven decades of facing the other across it. Switzerland's foreign minister Ignazio Cassis arrives on Thursday for two days to open the first dedicated Swiss political office in Pyongyang, the highest-level Western visit in years.

The Mecca pact's three signatories go to Tehran

Senior security officials from Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan met Iranian counterparts in Tehran on Monday, the first high-level contact between Iran and the three signatories of August's Mecca mutual defence pact since the war that began with US and Israeli strikes spread to Saudi Arabia and Yemen. The talks ran on the same day the Houthis launched fresh attacks on Saudi territory and Egypt approved defence support for the kingdom, so mediation and escalation are now moving at the same hour rather than in sequence. Donald Trump told reporters on Sunday that there would be no strike that day after earlier saying it might come the next, and Benjamin Netanyahu denied that the Israel Defense Forces had refused an order to attack over the weekend because of the election calendar. Brent's fall suggests traders are pricing a delay rather than a settlement, which is the defensible read while the US ambassador to the UN is still describing a war that could run another year. Europe's exposure sits in airspace closures, shipping insurance and refined-product flows rather than in the diplomacy, and none of those improve on a pause that nobody has underwritten.

One strait scared China into clean tech; two shocks have not moved Europe

Hu Jintao named the problem in 2003 and Chinese planners have built around it ever since: a single maritime chokepoint Beijing could not defend, which became the organising idea behind two decades of supply-chain policy and, eventually, the world's largest clean-technology base. Europe has absorbed two actual energy shocks in four years and is spending this autumn loosening the rules that would cut the same dependence, with member states delaying negotiations on the methane regulation on Monday. Italy and Poland lined up behind Ursula von der Leyen's joint gas purchasing while rejecting the climate conditions Brussels wants attached, which is a buyer-power argument rather than a demand-reduction one and leaves the volume unchanged. The Commission's other instruction this week runs the opposite way, telling capitals to write adaptation plans for 3C of warming and to take ownership of the damage from floods, heatwaves and fires themselves. The asymmetry is the whole story: Beijing converted one hypothetical scare into industrial capacity, while Europe has converted two real ones into procedure.

India's legacy publishers take the privacy law to the Supreme Court

Twenty-one publishers, among them Mukesh Ambani's Network18, Gautam Adani's NDTV, the Indian Express and Times Internet, have challenged the Modi government's privacy law before the Supreme Court, arguing in a 698-page filing that it would expose journalists' sources and reduce investigative work to what the Digital News Publishers Association calls pre-censorship. The signatory list is the substance: the houses usually closest to the government are the ones filing, in a country whose press freedom ranking has fallen steadily under Narendra Modi, and the matter is listed for hearing on Tuesday. The same court has issued notice to the Election Commission over the Special Intensive Revision of Maharashtra's electoral rolls, declining interim relief while telling voters whose names were deleted that they will not face prosecution for reapplying through Form 6. The currency is moving in the same direction, with the rupee weakening for a third session to 95.1850 against the dollar, roughly 0.8 percent across three days, and foreign portfolio investors having withdrawn Rs 3.08 lakh crore this year against Rs 1.66 lakh crore in all of 2025. Delhi is meanwhile widening its ethanol mandate to E85 and near-pure E100 for flex-fuel vehicles, which shifts demand off imported crude and onto groundwater and food-crop accounts the programme has not published.

The Commission rewiring reaches the directorates

Outside advisers working on Ursula von der Leyen's reorganisation of the European Commission have come back asking for a stronger centre, fewer bureaucratic silos and structural change inside some of the most powerful directorates-general. The weight of that lands on Secretary-General Ilze Juhansone, whose office would absorb coordinating authority that currently sits with individual services, which is a decision about where policy gets settled rather than an administrative tidy-up. Frontex is being rebuilt along the same axis, with new powers to carry out returns beyond the EU's borders and a requirement to be ready for a border crisis within 24 hours, drawn from the Ceuta episode. Austria has become the first capital to threaten a veto over the 2028 to 2034 budget, which is where each of these institutional ambitions eventually has to be financed. Lobbying tracks structure as reliably as money does: tobacco industry meetings in Brussels have quadrupled this parliamentary term against the last one while the EU rewrites its tobacco rules.

Mark Carney's middle-power alliance and von der Leyen's bid for influence are not the same project, and the Montreal text is where the difference becomes legible.

Korea's chip quarter outruns the rest of its economy

Real output expanded 0.97 percent quarter on quarter against the Bank of Korea's August forecast of 0.3 percent, with the gap almost entirely memory shipments and the capital spending attached to them. Consumption contracted over the same three months, which complicates the rate path rather than settling it, because an expansion carried by one export line gives the central bank no demand signal to keep tightening against. Seoul is assembling the fiscal counterpart now in a 162 trillion won Future Response Fund, about $122 billion, which routes the extra tax revenue from the cycle into long-term investment while holding reserves against the next downturn. Laura Alfaro, chief economist of the Inter-American Development Bank, argued in Seoul on Tuesday for exactly that treatment of the windfall, which is the standard commodity-exporter prescription applied to semiconductors. Samsung is the concrete version of the same cycle: Lee Jae-yong passed four years as chairman with the company guiding to its largest quarterly profit, a reversal from the slump that opened his tenure.

Beijing promotes the sector it spent a decade disciplining

The STAR 50 index of Shanghai-listed technology and innovation companies has fallen 40 percent since June and the larger mainland indices by about 20 percent, as investors start asking what the artificial intelligence build-out earns rather than what it costs. The policy contradiction behind the drawdown predates it: the 15th Five-Year Plan named gaming for the first time as both a cultural export and a site of technical innovation, and the National Press and Publishing Administration formalised recommendations in September to push domestic titles abroad, after a decade of curfews, real-name verification and facial-recognition checks that thinned the talent pipeline. What the state chose not to regulate says more than what it did: a December 2023 draft capping in-game spending was withdrawn within a month under market pressure while the restrictions on minors kept tightening. Robotics is running the same loop in miniature, with Unitree's shares up more than 60 percent in under two weeks while the company itself tells investors to be careful about the commercial prospects for humanoids. Subsidy plus discipline produces champions on paper and a thinner bench in every sector it is applied to.

Migration now carries what is left of population growth

Residents with a migrant background reached 2.81 million last November, 5.4 percent of the population, growing by 95,000 in a year while the total population rose by about 12,000.
