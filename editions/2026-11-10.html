Background on today's names

Person

Petteri Orpo

Finland · b. 1969

Prime Minister of Finland

National Coalition leader who has held interior and finance portfolios and took the premiership in 2023. He runs the government of the member state with the longest border with Russia.

Why it matters. Finland is among the net contributors bordering Russia that oppose Merz's move to tie the EU budget to Ukraine financing, warning that linking the two could jeopardise Ukraine's war effort.

Finland shares a border of about 1,340 kilometres with Russia, the longest of any EU member state.

Institution

Council of the EU

Council of the European Union · Brussels

The 27 governments' legislative chamber · it adopts the multiannual budget by unanimity

National ministers meet in configurations by policy area and share lawmaking with the European Parliament; on the long-term budget the Council decides unanimously once Parliament has given its consent.

Why it matters. Unanimity on the multiannual financial framework is the lever Merz is using: without Germany there is no budget, and he wants every capital behind Ukraine's financing first.

Its ministers meet in Luxembourg rather than Brussels in April, June and October.

Merz ties Germany's vote on the EU budget to Ukraine funding

Friedrich Merz has told António Costa in writing that unanimous backing for Ukraine's financing is a precondition for Germany approving the next multiannual financial framework (MFF), and he wants leaders to drop unanimity for future loans to Kyiv. He is pressing Péter Magyar, in Berlin this week with his foreign, defence, economy and finance ministers, to join the Ukraine loan if Hungary wants EU funds. Valdis Dombrovskis signalled that his options paper on Ukraine's financing next year will seek the agreement of all 27. Ireland supports the link; Finland and Estonia oppose it, Estonia arguing that Nordic and Baltic states already give more relative to their size.

Paris pins the supermarket freezer sabotage on the Revolutionary Guards

France attributes the cyberattacks of 11 and 12 October, which raised temperatures in fridges and freezers at about 700 food distribution sites and spoiled millions of euros of stock, to a cyber unit of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), citing a joint report by its intelligence and cyber agencies. Jean-Noël Barrot calls it the first large-scale sabotage attack on French territory and wants EU-level sanctions; Paris prosecutors have opened a case, and Tehran has not responded. Similar attacks hit other European countries last week, in the same week that more than 70 bomb warnings sent to airlines and newsrooms across Europe all turned out to be hoaxes.
