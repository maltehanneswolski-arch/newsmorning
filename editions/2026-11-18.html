Monetary policy and financial stability for the won

Europe will field seven negotiators in Ukraine talks, none of them Kallas

European leaders agreed last week that if Russia-Ukraine talks resume in Abu Dhabi later this month, Europe will be represented by the national security advisers of Germany, Britain, France, Italy and Poland plus two senior EU officials. Kaja Kallas, the EU's High Representative, is shut out, as are all foreign ministers; she now says ministers want a more direct role. Putin has so far refused any European presence, and Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov hinted on Friday that admission would come with conditions. Jared Kushner backs a European seat, and Trump may raise it with Putin at Thursday's Shenzhen summit.

Pentagon's Colby tells Kyiv its deep strikes are not working

At Tuesday's NATO meeting in Brussels, Pentagon policy chief Elbridge Colby said Ukrainian deep strikes aimed at the Russian population had neither brought the war closer to an end nor slowed Russia's advance, and singled out attacks on Wildberries warehouses that Kyiv says store drone parts and ammunition. Boris Pistorius called the criticism "quite a stupid thing"; British defence secretary Wes Streeting said it was not for allies to tell Ukraine how to fight. The row follows Ukraine's strike that brought down one of the two arches of the 19km Kerch road bridge to Crimea, using its own Flamingo missiles and Dutch Ruta weapons rather than the German Taurus Zelensky had asked for.
