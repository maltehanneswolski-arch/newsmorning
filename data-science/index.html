Nvidia told a congressman chip tracking can be done

Nvidia representatives told Representative Bill Foster that the anti-smuggling measures in the Chip Security Act could be implemented, although an industry group had called them “potentially infeasible”; the bill would require geotracking to verify where advanced AI chips end up. A forensic report by former OpenAI researcher Will DePue finds Blackwell signatures in published weights from DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group, and puts the hardware behind five training runs at more than half a billion dollars. Analysts in Singapore expect the Megaspeed case to narrow the city-state's room in the chip trade.

New York opens its register for frontier developers

New York opened a registration portal this week for frontier developers under its RAISE Act, which covers companies with more than $500 million in annual revenue and requires them to register by 1 January 2027. They must publish their safety frameworks, report critical safety incidents within 72 hours and release transparency reports on their frontier models. A new Office of Digital Innovation, Governance, Integrity, and Trust (DIGIT) enforces the law, with fines of up to $1 million for a first violation and $3 million after that, and Kathy Hochul has set up a Center for AI Responsibility and Research at Binghamton University to evaluate systems independently.

Anthropic's IPO turns away Chinese buyers

Banks running Anthropic's IPO are rejecting buy orders from mainland China and Hong Kong under the International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR), as they did for SpaceX's listing; Anthropic's products are not sold in either place anyway. Orders are about six times the shares on offer, the roadshow runs through Wednesday, when the $64 billion deal is expected to price, and trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT is due on 19 November. It is on course to be the largest IPO ever, though investors question how long Anthropic's lead will last.

Google ships Gemini 4 Hydrogen as its fast tier

Google DeepMind released Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a speed-first model beside Carbon and Argon, at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output, with four thinking levels, a 1M-token context window and a free tier in AI Studio. Artificial Analysis scores it 54 on its intelligence index at the new xhigh level, one point behind Claude Sonnet 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol, with an 8% hallucination rate against their 43% and 52%; it trails both on Terminal-Bench 4.0. One tester saw xhigh burn 131,000 thinking tokens without answering.
