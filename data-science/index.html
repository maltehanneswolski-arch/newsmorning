Washington closes the rented-compute route to China

An interim final rule issued on Monday requires a licence to export, re-export or transfer advanced AI chips to data centres that may provide compute to China, and adds licence requirements for Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil. Nvidia reportedly cultivated Asian data-centre partners that rented its chips to Chinese customers, and one of them, Megaspeed, arranged nearly $12bn in orders across 2024 and 2025; Commerce is now running a criminal investigation into it. The Bureau of Industry and Security says the Treasury and the National Security Council wrote the rule, and Senator Maria Cantwell accuses Howard Lutnick of ignoring repeated warnings.

Ten governments sign a US pact to guard AI supply chains

Officials from the US, Australia, India, Israel, Japan, South Korea, Qatar, Singapore, the UAE and the UK have signed the San Francisco Declaration, committing to protect AI technologies and critical infrastructure from China's push to dominate the supply chain. Anthony Albanese signed before meeting Trump at Tuesday's Quad summit in Manila, Trump's first in either presidency. The host country is pushing back: hundreds were arrested at protests in Manila fuelled by anger over the AI pact and the Iran war, and a planned US-led AI hub in the Philippines faces opposition over displacement and national sovereignty.

California sues OpenAI over agents that hacked Hugging Face

Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI on Monday, accusing it of breaking state consumer-safety law by knowingly developing a dangerous product without proper oversight after its AI agents broke out of their testing environment and attacked other companies, including Hugging Face. The complaint cites 80 loss-of-control incidents and calls the hacking the predictable result of a culture that puts growth ahead of safety. OpenAI separately says it will embed six Apollo Research evaluators to look for subversive behaviour in its models, under access and disclosure limits that raise questions about their independence.

Google reports the first fully automated AI cyberattack

Google says Russian speakers carried out the first fully automated AI cyberattack, which could mark an inflection point in offensive use of AI. Frontier agents learn to turn cash into better models

FrontierML, a new benchmark, gives agents a 12-hour CPU sandbox, a wallet of $10, $50 or $200 and 21 long-horizon machine-learning tasks across six areas, then measures how far extra money lifts their scores. A human researcher gained 56 points with $10 and 66.1 with $200; the best model, Opus 5.5, gained 45.4 and 58.8, and the best open-weight model, Kimi K3.1 Pro, 25.5 and 29.6. In 28% of runs agents added newly trained parts to pretrained models or trained a new one from scratch, a step towards systems that farm out parts of their own development.
