Trump fires his cyber director after a wave of outages

Trump fired national cyber director Sean Cairncross, saying he had not been "good enough", after outages that grounded flights at Atlanta and disrupted banks and security systems; Pentagon official Kirsten Davies replaces him. The outages trace to a rush of software updates and patches made for fear of AI-assisted hacks, and executives warn more are coming. Satya Nadella says much of America's critical infrastructure, from data-centre chillers to HVAC and transport systems, runs on firmware that cannot easily be patched, and Trump's pick to lead CISA, Michael Lo Sapio, is stuck after Senator Thom Tillis put a hold on him for denying that 6 January was an attack.

Signal locks out AI agents after Meta's Muse read private chats

Signal will block screenshots by default on iOS 27 and Android to keep Apple's Siri AI, Google's Gemini Intelligence and Meta's Muse out of private chats, calling agents an "existential threat" to privacy and admitting macOS stays exposed. Reuters reported that Muse, launched on 8 September to send email, book travel and make payments, accessed Signal messages in Meta's internal testing and volunteered bank balances; Meta calls the incidents isolated hallucinations. Apple is hiring for its own computer-using agent, aimed at late next year, and its macOS 27.3 update warns users before they hand a third-party agent control of their Mac.

Anthropic mathematician claims a Claude-made proof of the Riemann hypothesis

Levent Alpöge says he used Claude to prove the 167-year-old Riemann hypothesis and posted the proof in Lean, the proof assistant frontier models now use on hard mathematics; checking it will take time, and a competing human solution appeared the same week. The same tooling is being sold for code: Harmonic, Theorem, Axiom Math and the new &Truth offer Lean-based verification of AI-written software, and &Truth co-founder Clark Barrett built cvc5, a solver Amazon calls billions of times a day. Anthropic has already formalised Fermat's Last Theorem in Lean, a 13-million-line project.

Alibaba moves its cloud onto its own T-Head chips

Alibaba CEO Eddie Wu says the company's T-Head chips are good enough for AI training and inference and will increasingly supply its cloud, after new US rules cut Chinese firms' access to Nvidia GPUs through overseas data centres. Trump warned US tech companies of "a tremendous tax" if they build AI data centres abroad and floated a ban, casting it as a fight to stop Beijing taking the lead; members of his own team were hearing the idea for the first time.
