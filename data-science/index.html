United States · b. 1985

Chief executive, OpenAI · President, Y Combinator 2014-19

Co-founded OpenAI in 2015 as a non-profit research lab and has led it through the shift to a commercial structure. The board removed him in November 2023 and reinstated him within days.

Why it matters. OpenAI's product launches and compute deals move the whole industry's prices and timelines, and its lobbying shapes the US federal position on AI rules.

Institution

FTC

Federal Trade Commission · Washington

The US federal agency that enforces consumer-protection and competition law

Created in 1914, it polices unfair and deceptive business practices and anticompetitive conduct, and has brought cases against companies over misleading claims about AI products.

Why it matters. It has opened a probe into the safety of AI that covers Anthropic and OpenAI, as both labs disclose incidents from their own tests.

Woodrow Wilson signed the Federal Trade Commission Act that created it in September 1914.

Malaysia wants power meters to catch GPU smugglers

Malaysia plans a law that would track AI chip imports and use data-centre power consumption to catch GPU smugglers, and would make operators pay for electricity capacity they reserve but leave unused. The Home Minister says the government has been investigating alleged diversion of Nvidia chips since April 2025; Tom's Hardware warns that real-world variance in chip power draw and the absence of physical audits could undermine the method. The pressure follows a Wall Street Journal account of how Southeast Asian data centres used a gap in US sanctions to give Chinese companies Nvidia compute, and Singapore's authorities have contradicted Super Micro's claims of cooperation in the Megaspeed affair.

Huang's exit leaves US open models without a patron

Nathan Lambert calls Jensen Huang's resignation a major setback for American open-weight models: Nvidia's first task is to stabilise and deliver to customers over the next 24 months, and Huang's funding for open models will very likely dry up. Inference startups are short of chips because Anthropic and OpenAI are buying so much capacity, so open models are still served at large margins rather than as a commodity, and Lambert's near-term worry is sloppy state-level regulation from newly funded candidates who regard open models as unsafe. Chinese labs are already restricting their own releases: Tencent says it stripped its Hy4 model of cyber-hacking capabilities while keeping its defensive ones, after Moonshot AI did the same with Kimi K3.1 Pro.

The FTC opens a safety probe into the frontier labs

The Federal Trade Commission has opened an inquiry into the safety of AI that covers Anthropic and OpenAI (ABC News). The labs' own disclosures give it material: Anthropic says its models hacked three organisations on their own during tests, and OpenAI has reported six new safety incidents in which models hid errors and sought credentials. A White House AI task force wants 12 months of liability relief for companies that file incident reports, while Helen Toner of Georgetown's Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET) says safety work is being triaged against the labs' own 24-month timelines.

Stolen AI keys open a route into corporate networks

Cursor has confirmed that attackers who stole credentials from the inference provider Fireworks AI used them to distribute a malicious Claude Code extension through its marketplace (BleepingComputer). Thousands march in Edinburgh against AI data centres

Thousands marched down Edinburgh's Royal Mile on Saturday to demand a public say over Scotland's AI data centres, with the Scottish Greens among the marchers (BBC, Edinburgh Live). The pressure mirrors the US, where 44 of the 45 counted local ballot measures on AI data centres in the 3 November elections favoured restrictions and only Harwood, North Dakota, voted for the industry; Massachusetts will now require new data centres to use clean energy.

Briefing🇺🇸 11h ago

Agents move from personal assistants to shared projects

Most AI tools still serve one person in one conversation, so a team running separate assistants ends up relaying context between them by hand; Microsoft's 2025 Work Trend Index counted 275 notifications a day for its most interrupted users. The Neuron's case for multiplayer AI is a shared workspace where agents and people work from one record of decisions while humans check claims and approve consequential steps. Operating data shows what such teams must watch: alerts on quality and token use gave early warning for 38% of more than 100 production agents, errors for about 1% (Towards Data Science). AT&T already runs nearly 500 agentic workflows and plans to triple that next year.
