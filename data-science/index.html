Know the players

Background on today's names

Person 🇺🇸

Dario Amodei

United States · b. 1983

Co-founder and chief executive, Anthropic

Led research at OpenAI before leaving in 2021 with colleagues to found Anthropic, which builds the Claude models. Trained as a physicist, with a doctorate in biophysics from Princeton.

Why it matters. He signs the S-1 that put Anthropic's $895.4 billion of compute commitments and its own warning about partner financing on the public record ahead of the Nasdaq debut.

Before OpenAI he worked on speech recognition at Baidu and on research at Google Brain.

Institution 🇺🇸

Anthropic

Anthropic · San Francisco

AI company and public benefit corporation that builds the Claude models

Founded in 2021 by former OpenAI researchers, it publishes a responsible scaling policy tying model releases to safety evaluations.

Why it matters. Its updated S-1 is the first time a frontier lab has set out quarterly revenue, losses and long-term compute obligations in a securities filing, where misstatement carries liability.

It was founded by seven people who left OpenAI together.

Anthropic's S-1 shows $17 billion a quarter in revenue and $895 billion in compute owed

Anthropic's updated S-1 reports $17 billion of Q3 revenue against $1.4 billion a year earlier, with quarterly growth slowing to 47% from 144% and the net loss narrowing to $23.3 billion from $66.3 billion in Q2 [Matt from FutureTools]. It also lists $895.4 billion in purchase and lease commitments, almost all for computing, with about $69.8 billion due in the next 12 months. South Korean brokerages are sitting the offering out after a SpaceX setback, which may leave Korean retail investors without shares before the Nasdaq debut [The Korea Herald].

Google ties school Search to its AI tools

From early next year, K-12 student accounts will start with Search, Gemini and Gemini Notebook all switched off, so a district that keeps AI out of classrooms also loses Google Search [The Rundown AI, TLDR]. New York and Los Angeles restricted AI for about 1 million students this semester. The announcement came a day after the 2 November deadline senators set for Google to answer questions on AI in schools, and former FTC chair Lina Khan called it the use of a search monopoly to coerce schools.
