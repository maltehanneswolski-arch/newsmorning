OpenAI's chief scientist proposes an industry speed limit

Jakub Pachocki wants AI companies to agree limits on developing more powerful models until researchers have better ways to understand and monitor their behaviour, with the limit pegged to a shared metric of model capability; Sam Altman backed the proposal on X. METR, which evaluates frontier systems, says models such as Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra are improving at research tasks faster than they can be reliably measured, and current data cannot rule out a dangerous acceleration, though common tests may also overstate what the models can do. A voluntary pact would bind only the labs that sign it.

Anthropic's listing pays Amazon and Google twice

Anthropic priced its Nasdaq listing on Thursday at $83 a share; each of its seven founders sold about $280 million of stock and still holds roughly $20 billion on paper. Amazon's and Google's disclosed stakes were worth about $161 billion and $147 billion at that price, the shares carry no votes, and Anthropic has committed to spend at least $110 billion with Amazon from May 2026 to April 2036, largely regardless of usage; underwriters took $550 million in fees. Outside the exchange, protesters asked for safety rules, limits on environmental and labour harm and no bailout if the bubble bursts, and the founders' pledge of 80 percent of their equity to philanthropy is not binding.

Investors read the AI sell-off as a verdict on overbuilt compute

The S&P 500 entered correction territory on Friday, and on Monday the rout reached Asia: Hong Kong hit a 19-month low, the KOSPI fell 5 percent and Taiwan banned short sales below the previous close after its worst week since 1991. Mark MacCarthy and David Klaus argue the crash reflects investors recognising overinvestment rather than fear of regulation, and that the political backlash against data centres may force a slowdown in data-centre growth just as oversupply becomes plain. Customers have balked at frontier prices and moved between providers, including cheap Chinese models, while server makers seek price rises of up to 45 percent on signed contracts as memory costs climb.

US chip rules strand Southeast Asia's neocloud boom

Washington's new export-control rulebook reaches data centres in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia, threatening a multibillion-dollar cloud business that rented compute to Chinese firms and leaving some projects at risk of being abandoned unless their operators find new customers. Banks have pulled back from financing Chinese neoclouds, and Trump still wants only older-generation chips sold to China, leaving Nvidia's H200 exports in limbo. The rules land as imports of Singapore-made chipmaking equipment into three Chinese hubs doubled to $3.46 billion in the first seven months of 2026, and as a Chinese national, Li Ming, faces fraud charges in Singapore for allegedly misrepresenting where servers bought for Megaspeed were going.

Tencent lets outside evaluators test Hy4 before release

Tencent worked with an international group of AI safety organisations to assess the risk of its open-weight Hy4 model before releasing it, the first time a leading Chinese tech company has disclosed third-party evaluation of a new model ahead of release. The assessment comes amid concern over AI-assisted cyberattacks and biological misuse, and Tencent's report rates Hy4's hacking capabilities as limited. With the weights open, that pre-release check is the main one the model gets before it is in wide use.
