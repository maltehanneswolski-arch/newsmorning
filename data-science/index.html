Background on today's names

Person

Howard Lutnick

United States · b. 1961

US Secretary of Commerce, whose department writes and enforces export controls on AI chips

He ran the financial firm Cantor Fitzgerald for three decades, rebuilding it after it lost 658 employees in the 11 September 2001 attacks, and co-chaired Donald Trump's 2024 transition before taking over Commerce in February 2025.

Why it matters. Axios reports he stalled curbs on companies suspected of diverting Nvidia chips to China after Trump approved them for the third time this year, and Republicans in Congress have turned on him over it.

His brother Gary was among the Cantor Fitzgerald staff killed in the North Tower.

Washington moves to close the overseas route to Nvidia chips

The Commerce Department is finalizing a rule to stop Chinese AI firms reaching advanced chips remotely through data centres abroad and to curb smuggling, and Chinese labs are rushing to rent Nvidia capacity outside China before it lands. Alice Huang, the former Megaspeed executive at the centre of the Jensen Huang affair, allegedly obtained 240,000 restricted chips worth $7.5bn through a network in Singapore and Malaysia, and has returned to Beijing. Senators Elizabeth Warren and Chris Coons want every document on Nvidia's China exports preserved, Congress plans to call Huang to testify, and Microsoft has overtaken Nvidia in market value.

Google puts Gemini 4 Carbon and a decision model on its API

Gemini 4 Carbon, Google's strongest model, is open to all paying developers and enterprises through AI Studio and Gemini for business at $2 per million input and $10 per million output tokens; Google says it ranks first on Cursor Bench 4.0 and Zapier's Automation Bench at under half the cost per task of the next external model. Gemini Decision 1 scores closed-set choices in one pass instead of generating text: up to 1,000 questions per request, 255 options, 256K context, $0.10 per million input tokens and nothing for output. Artificial Analysis puts it at 66 on its Decision Index, level with Elephant 9B and Jev 1.14, seven points behind Drex Max, and slower than Jev at 211 against 92 milliseconds per task.

ChatGPT opens a storefront for brands

OpenAI's Businesses beta lets companies claim a verified profile in ChatGPT, load product feeds with prices and availability, and see how often the chatbot mentions them and sends clicks to their site. US online retailers and digital service firms can also launch Business Agents that answer shoppers in a labelled chat kept apart from ChatGPT's own answers, and brands can now pay to make their agent the landing spot for ads in ChatGPT. OpenAI first piloted ad-linked agents in September, and profiles are free to claim for eligible companies.

Brussels proposes the Digital Fairness Act

The European Commission's Digital Fairness Act targets dark patterns, hard-to-cancel subscriptions, addictive design and hidden fees, would let users switch off streaks and autoplay, and makes influencers disclose when they are paid. A separate reform of consumer enforcement would let Brussels fine companies up to 6% of annual global turnover. Lawyers read the proposal as reaching AI agents that buy on consumers' behalf, European gaming firms say its curbs on paying to keep playing will cripple them, and the law scholar Christoph Busch expects member states to take time to agree it.

Finland and Estonia want an EU system for serious AI incidents

In a letter before Friday's informal summit in Dublin, the first time EU leaders debate AI, Finland and Estonia call for EU-level arrangements to respond to serious AI incidents. The meeting was meant to cover AI alone until Giorgia Meloni asked for the Ukraine envoy question to be added this week.
