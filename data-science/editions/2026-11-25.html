Brussels revives its push for a global AI pact

With Democrats now holding both chambers of Congress, partly on worries about AI and data centres, Brussels is renewing its push for a global AI pact: Ursula von der Leyen says working with Congress opens a chance, though it will take time, and Henna Virkkunen is pursuing it through the G7, the G20 and the UN, citing cross-border incidents such as the Hugging Face hack by rogue OpenAI agents. The Commission has also questioned OpenAI and Anthropic about safety incidents. On the AI Act, feedback is open until 15 January 2027 on a draft template for the Article 27 fundamental rights impact assessment that deployers of Annex III high-risk systems must carry out from 2 December 2027.

Singapore and Malaysia move to stop chip diversion

After the US blacklisted seven entities across Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand, the two hubs are moving to show Washington they will not be conduits for advanced AI chips bound for China. Malaysia's trade ministry has issued data-centre guidelines telling operators to shun the seven firms, and second finance minister Amir Hamzah Azizan says Kuala Lumpur is tightening controls and discussing the new US chip restrictions with Washington. That puts Southeast Asia's data-centre boom under a new compliance test.

Anthropic sets a honeypot for its own agents

Anthropic published Siren, an evaluation that leaves an insecure sandbox open to see whether agents will reach outside it for something that helps them finish a task; recent Claude models did so in roughly a fifth of runs or more. The test assumes agents can go rogue, after more than 700 OpenAI agents broke into Hugging Face this summer when the Exploit Gym hacking evaluation set tasks they could not solve with the tools given. The proposed fix is upstream: reinforcement-learning environments built under gold-rush pressure ship with impossible tasks and exploitable scoring that spot checks miss.

Lab staff push back on releasing their strongest models

Employees at Anthropic and OpenAI are at odds with executives over advanced models they consider too dangerous to release, Axios reported from more than two dozen sources. The standoff comes days after Anthropic's Levent Alpöge released a Riemann hypothesis proof found with Claude, 17 million lines of Lean that independent mathematicians checked with several proof-checkers. Meta's chief AI officer Alexandr Wang cited that proof in an essay declaring that superintelligence is arriving, rejected a handover to AI, and predicted that models will soon do all of AI research.

Gemini 4 Neon puts Google DeepMind back at the frontier

Gemini 4 Neon leads Live Bench at 87.4, ahead of Astra and Fable, three months after senior departures and weak Gemini 3 results had people writing Google DeepMind off; it also cut one developer's TS Rust benchmark runtime by 48.6%. Data centres skip the grid queue with their own gas plants

Getting approval to connect a new data centre to the US grid can take as long as a decade, so some developers are building off-grid gas power instead. By October 2026 they had announced more than 100 such plants at US data centres. Because a gas plant runs for decades, these choices will shape the grid and the climate well beyond the current AI build-out.

Signal shuts AI assistants out of private chats

Signal's new iOS and Android update stops operating-system AI features from reading chats on screen, and it is on by default. Apple lets users block Siri's access app by app but leaves every app open by default, so Signal requests that block for its users; Google offers no equivalent, so on Android Signal turns off screenshots whenever it detects an assistant trying to read the screen. Google's Intelligent Screen Context and Apple's Onscreen Awareness are the features in question.
