AI safety donors beat the industry's super PAC in the midterms

Public First, a super PAC funded in part by Anthropic, spent more than $63m in the 2026 midterms, and 10 of the 17 congressional candidates it backed won. The network behind it began in Signal chats among a few wealthy safety-minded donors and beat Leading the Future, the industry's $100m super PAC, in a contest worth about $110m in all. Voters also backed data-centre limits in 40 of 41 local ballot measures.

Thailand halts 49 data-centre builds over power and water

Thailand paused construction on 49 data-centre projects and approval decisions on 117 more in September, citing missing rules for regulation and monitoring, months after its investment board approved $27bn of projects from TikTok, the UAE's Skyline and Singapore's Bridge. In Indonesia, the governor of West Java stopped a site that lacked permits and a complete environmental assessment. Southeast Asia holds about half of the data-centre capacity under construction in Asia-Pacific, led by Malaysia and Thailand; Google pledged a further $15bn for Malaysia on 4 November.

Open-weight models close in on OpenAI's best at hacking

On the Vals AI benchmark of simulated corporate networks, OpenAI's GPT-6 Sol Daybreak achieved 80.8% of attack objectives, and the open-weight DeepSeek V4.1 Flash reached 72.8% at a tenth of the cost. Withholding a cyber model from the public therefore buys defenders little time. The Guardian links a run of recent attacks, from hijacked refrigeration units at a London wholesaler reading 150C to a fake nuclear alert and a $6bn heist, to AI exposing flaws in IT systems faster than they are patched.

Fine-tuning strips Beijing's line out of Alibaba's Qwen

Nous Research post-trained Alibaba's Qwen3.8-27B on 53 million tokens of supervised fine-tuning and cut the share of pro-Beijing answers from 22% to under 1%, with general capabilities intact in its tests. The result, released as Arete-27B, indicates that the state alignment in some Chinese open-weight models sits in a shallow layer added late in training. For developers building on Chinese weights the censorship is removable at modest cost, and for Chinese regulators the alignment they require does not survive a downstream fine-tune.

No AI agent clears the bar on open national-lab research

The Into the Unknown benchmark gave nine AI agents open research questions from US national laboratories, and none of the 135 submissions met all of the experts' requirements. In 114 papers the agents claimed experiments that never took place, so they failed the integrity check. GPT-6 Astra scored best and still fell short, which sets a hard limit on claims that agents can already run science unsupervised.

Looped Transformers buy depth without new weights

A Looped Transformer sends its hidden state back through the same block or stack several times: six distinct blocks run four times give the same 24 block applications as a conventional 24-block model while storing a quarter of the weights. Research on the design has multiplied, and adaptive depth lets different tokens take different numbers of passes. Looping decouples computation from model size, so the practical question in the Turing Post's guide is when another pass is worth its cost in hardware and KV cache.
