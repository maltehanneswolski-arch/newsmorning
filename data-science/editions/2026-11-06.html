Background on today's names

Person

Gina Hinojosa

United States · Texas

Governor-elect of Texas · Democratic member of the Texas House of Representatives for Austin

An Austin Democrat, she has sat in the Texas House since 2017 after leading the Austin school board, and ran for governor on a promise of more community say over where data centres are built.

Why it matters. She plans to suspend new data-centre approvals in a state that holds 22.8% of planned US capacity until Texas writes safeguards for residents, the grid and the environment.

She will be the first Democrat to govern Texas since Ann Richards left office in 1995.

Texas joins the data-centre freeze, and OpenAI rewrites its terms overnight

Voters opposed data centres in 40 of 41 midterm ballot measures and six cities banned them outright. Democrats flipped five governorships in states holding 37% of planned US capacity, and Texas, with 22.8%, now has a governor-elect, Gina Hinojosa, who plans to suspend new approvals until the state sets safeguards for residents, power and the environment; the incoming governors of Georgia and Ohio promise pauses too. OpenAI answered within a day with a framework it wants written into law, dropping nondisclosure agreements in site deals and paying the full cost of electricity and infrastructure so residents do not, while Meta warned that the backlash could make expansion harder.

Lenders and buyers pull back from the AI build-out

The Nasdaq fell 5.5% on Thursday, its worst day in 19 months, and slid into correction with more than $2tn erased, as investors bet that a Democratic Congress will curb AI data centres; Seoul's exchange triggered a circuit breaker on Friday. The strain is reaching credit: the cost of insuring SoftBank, which has a reported $64.6bn in OpenAI, hit a four-year high, and ING put $500m of data-centre loans up for sale, including $25m tied to a Stargate site in New Mexico. Buyers are trimming too: Qualcomm's profit forecast disappointed as memory prices rose, and Apple cut engineers' daily AI allowances from $300 to $200, with further cuts to as little as $100 planned.

Google and OpenAI hand their strongest models to defenders first

Gemini 4 Carbon now tops the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 59, narrowly ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 and cheaper per task, yet Google is giving it to select cybersecurity companies first, as it did with Gemini 4 Argon, and has set no date for wider release. OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber with a warning of critical security risks; it beat GPT-5.6 Cyber in exploit testing. Two counterweights: SemiAnalysis's new GDPx benchmark of simulated knowledge work finds no agent scoring above 12%, and Ripple-65B Preview, an open-weight model, leads Terminal Bench 2.1 against open models many times its size.
