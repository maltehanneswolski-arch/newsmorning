Colette Kress takes over a leaderless Nvidia

Nvidia's chief financial officer Colette Kress is interim chief executive after Jensen Huang resigned on Thursday as chief executive, president and director over affairs that include one with a woman US officials suspect of spying for China. The shares lost about half a trillion dollars of value in a week; Huang keeps 3.6% of the company and Donald Trump has proposed Tim Cook as successor. Semafor's Reed Albergotti expects a more profitable and less inventive Nvidia at a time when OpenAI designed its own chip in months, and Korean memory makers may gain leverage in HBM (high-bandwidth memory) supply talks.

Chip hawks go after the Megaspeed route

Lawmakers want Howard Lutnick to explain why Commerce allowed chip sales to a company linked to the alleged spy after he stalled export curbs, and the White House is discussing his future; China has signalled retaliation if the curbs pass. Malaysia is investigating Speed Matrix, a Megaspeed subsidiary renting data-centre space there, and Singapore has charged a fifth person, Shi Bingzhe, with 41 counts. Procurement notices show Northwestern Polytechnical University bought four H200s through a local supplier, and Chinese cloud firms are trying to lock in Nvidia capacity abroad. Nvidia privately told Representative Bill Foster that the tracking required by the Chip Security Act is technically possible, while an industry group calls it potentially infeasible.

Chinese experts want a standing AI channel with Washington

China and the US agreed in September to open an AI Dialogue and a channel for AI incidents, the next round is due this month, and AI cooperation may be a deliverable when Xi Jinping and Donald Trump meet at APEC in Shenzhen next week. Five Chinese experts, including Tsinghua's Xiao Qian and Tongji's Lu Chuanying, say the realistic aim is a regular channel insulated from shocks, with incident reporting run by professional teams under strict confidentiality. They see common ground on cyber, bio and loss-of-control risks and on criminal misuse, and expect China to write global rules on equal terms beside the UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance. Analysts quoted by SCMP want a hotline against AI-induced nuclear attacks.

AlphaGenome 2 predicts what a DNA typo does in each cell type

Google DeepMind's AlphaGenome 2 takes a stretch of one million DNA letters plus a tissue, cell type or cell state and predicts what that cell does with it, after training on more than 100 million single-cell profiles across 68 human and mouse tissues. It finds nearly twice as many gene-changing variants in cells as the previous best model, scores 30% against 16% on causal variants and 43% against 23% on effect direction across 28 immune cell types, and adapts to a new cell state from a few dozen cells without retraining. The figures are DeepMind's own; the model is for research only, free for non-commercial use through an API, with a private preview for commercial work.

United locks AI agents out of its bookings

United Airlines has barred unauthorised third-party AI agents from its app and website and from booking through travel agencies and aggregators, citing exposed logins, accidental purchases, misspent MileagePlus miles and aviation rules; chief executive Scott Kirby says he does not want another middleman. Amazon has asked Perplexity to pull shopping from its Comet browser, blocked Meta's Muse agent and pushes its own Buy for Me tool. Walmart took the opposite line with Agent Profiles, which give outside assistants permissions, spending limits and per-purchase approval, though not every feature is confirmed live.

EU leaders hold their first AI debate with Nvidia in turmoil

EU leaders meeting informally in Dublin on Friday have AI at the top of the agenda for the first time, in a debate squeezed by Ukraine and the budget fight. Europe buys almost all its frontier models and the Nvidia hardware beneath them from the US or China, and several leaders courted Huang on his European tour last month to secure supply and investment. The Netherlands, home of ASML, has most to watch in the fallout, while the EU institutions themselves are struggling with the AI workload.
