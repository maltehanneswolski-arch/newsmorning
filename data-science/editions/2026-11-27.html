Chip curbs freeze Southeast Asia's AI build-out

Nvidia has told partners such as Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, after a US rule effective 19 November began requiring licences for advanced AI chips bought by companies there. Dealers are hunting buyers for thousands of B300 chips, and sellers now refuse customers whose computing capacity could be rented to Chinese firms, a loophole the rule was meant to close. Fitch says the rules threaten data-centre growth across Asia-Pacific, and Singapore's deputy prime minister Gan Kim Yong says his government is talking to Washington to keep access.

Amodei rejects handing society's decisions to AI

Dario Amodei called the idea that he wants humans to surrender free will to AI absurd and said the technology should be governed through legitimate democratic processes. He separated delegating tasks, such as driving a car, designing a cancer drug or writing software, from letting AI write the rules people live under, and said slowing or stopping some deployments may make sense to limit job disruption and AI's ability to build more powerful AI on its own. He also accused rivals of using slogans about human control without tackling the risks, a day after Axios reported that staff at Anthropic and OpenAI privately worry some unreleased models are too powerful to release safely.

FCC clears a million SpaceX data-centre satellites

The FCC has authorised SpaceX to deploy up to 1 million Starmind satellites that would process AI workloads in orbit, which the agency says will help meet demand for computing. The licence requires deployment to start by 2033, and SpaceX wants the constellation operating by 2040; Google and Blue Origin are also working on orbital compute, though Boston Consulting Group expects costs to stay well above ground-based data centres for now. Scientists warn the satellites could overcrowd orbit and obscure the stars, and Europe has opened a megafactory to build a rival to Starlink.

Google splits coding work between Gemini 4 models

Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen on 12 November for fast, cheaper coding and agentic work, then Gemini 4 Neon on 18 November, which rivals Carbon at intermediate speed and cost. Hydrogen runs at 173 tokens per second and costs $1.50/$7.50 per million input/output tokens; Neon runs at 99 tokens per second for $3/$15. Its coding agent Antigravity gained Orchestrator, which keeps one agent in one context window but lets Carbon or Neon plan a task and Hydrogen execute it, handing control back when a check fails twice. Parameter counts, training data and how the smaller models were adapted from Carbon are undisclosed, The Batch notes.

Bailey warns unreleased models put encryption at risk

Bank of England governor Andrew Bailey says unreleased AI models have raised concerns about encryption, after breakthroughs in mathematics stoked fears that data across the financial system is under threat. The Bank is cutting capital requirements for banks while telling lenders they must keep pace with the scale and sophistication of cyber threats, and Bailey argues the technology needs boundaries, a bit like rearing children. The warning lands days after AI-assisted proofs of open mathematical problems, which complexity theorist Scott Aaronson says matter less than the fact that a machine can now produce them.

Microsoft maps the ARTEX attack framework

Microsoft has published the architecture of ARTEX, an AI framework in wide use since July that runs cyberattacks through specialised agents with almost no human involvement and can breach weak defences in minutes. Its threat intelligence report names the Chinese state groups Salt Typhoon and Crystal Typhoon and the North Korean groups Onyx Sleet and Pearl Sleet as users, alongside criminal organisations. Switzerland's government separately says AI agents were used in five cyberattacks there.

SAP prepares to cut 10,000 jobs for its AI overhaul

SAP is preparing to eliminate at least 10,000 positions, about 9% of its global workforce, to rebuild its software business for AI, with product, engineering and sales teams in focus. It would be the group's second major round of layoffs in about two years, and the scope is still subject to change. In Germany the works council has negotiated an employment guarantee through 2028.
