Background on today's names

Person

Emmanuel Macron

France · b. 1977

President of the French Republic

A former investment banker and economy minister, he founded En Marche in 2016, won the presidency in 2017 and was re-elected in 2022. France hosted the AI Action Summit in Paris in February 2025.

Why it matters. He convened the Paris Call for AI Safety and Security, which commits signatory labs to shutdown mechanisms and independent audits, and wants AI banned wherever it threatens children.

He became president at 39, the youngest French head of state since Napoleon.

Labs sign the Paris Call on shutdown mechanisms

Eight countries and ten AI companies, OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft and Mistral among them, signed the Paris Call for AI Safety and Security at a meeting Emmanuel Macron hosted on Monday. The companies commit to shutdown mechanisms, independent audits and disclosure of known risks, but the text sets no binding sanctions or deadlines, and a safety group says it lacks teeth. The UAE has joined the French declaration on protecting children, and Macron wants AI banned wherever it threatens them as France drafts a charter on AI risks to citizens.

New York starts enforcing its frontier AI law

New York has begun enforcing its AI safety law, putting OpenAI, Anthropic and other large developers under new oversight, and the state will run its own tests of the models. Scott Wiener, the author of California's advanced-AI safety legislation, is heading to the House and says Congress must slow AI down, though he is only cautiously optimistic it will. In the next Congress, Republican Pat Harrigan expects political headwinds for emerging technologies, and Democrat Ben Ray Luján wants the two parties to work together on quantum.

Anthropic sets its IPO range below the $2 trillion talk

Anthropic's filing on Tuesday prices shares at $70 to $78 for a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion, short of the $2 trillion widely reported before investors cooled on AI; it could list on Nasdaq next week and raise up to $64 billion. Revenue reached $33.2 billion in the first nine months against about $2.5 billion a year earlier, with an operating loss of $1.26 billion, and compute commitments total an estimated $895 billion. Goldman Sachs expects revenue above $600 billion by 2030, and TSMC's board approved up to $500 million for shares at the IPO price plus up to $500 million of OpenAI preferred stock.

The data-centre backlash is about electricity

In Ashburn, Virginia, the world's largest data-centre hub, opposition splits on whether a site draws power from the grid or generates its own, and each choice provokes its own blowback. After last week's midterms Microsoft and Sabey are preparing for new rules in Washington state, and OpenAI has pledged that residents will not bear the extra electricity costs of its projects. NextDC's chief executive promised to put communities first after local opposition delayed a Melbourne project last month and sank its shares; experts say Japan is not immune. The EIA (Energy Information Administration) expects US power demand to set records in 2026 and 2027.
