Institution

US Department of Commerce

United States Department of Commerce · Washington

The federal department for trade, standards and export controls, headed by Howard Lutnick

It houses the Bureau of Industry and Security, which enforces export controls and the Entity List, alongside NIST, the Census Bureau and the trade-remedy authorities.

Why it matters. Lutnick faces questions in Congress and from Treasury over a softer line on Nvidia's China sales, and the chip bills gathering support would tighten the controls his department enforces.

It was founded in 1903 as the Department of Commerce and Labor; labour got a department of its own in 1913.

Congress lines up chip export bills after Huang's exit

Jensen Huang's resignation over an affair with a suspected Chinese spy has given bipartisan momentum to tighter AI chip export rules, including a bill to block Chinese companies from reaching US chips overseas. Megaspeed, the Singapore firm where Alice Huang worked, arranged nearly $12 billion of Nvidia orders for Chinese companies in 2024-25, the WSJ reports, and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick faces questions over a softer line on Nvidia's China sales. Huang's exit also removes the most influential industry advocate of open AI systems, which the FT reads as a gain for Anthropic and OpenAI.

Oakland workers win a retraining guarantee if AI takes their jobs

Oakland city workers are voting on a three-year contract, tentatively agreed with IFPTE Local 21 on 31 October, that obliges the city to retrain or reassign anyone AI displaces; it carries a 9% raise and called off a strike planned for 5 November. Other public employers have conceded similar terms: Berkeley Unified must give 60 days' notice before introducing AI and may not use it to evaluate or discipline staff, and Columbus's transit agency needs union consent to put self-driving vehicles into service. A New York state law already shields covered public workers from losing jobs, hours or pay because their employer uses AI.
