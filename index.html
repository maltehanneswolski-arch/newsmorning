Shenzhen declaration shields Putin from the ICC and skips Ukraine

The "Declaration toward Peace" that Xi Jinping, Vladimir Putin and Donald Trump issued after their first trilateral in Shenzhen recycles the 1985 line that "nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought", warns the International Criminal Court (ICC) off and never mentions Ukraine or Iran. Its stated priorities are UN reform, more refined-oil supply, the protection of diplomatic immunity and a dialogue on nuclear risks; Russia and China had shown little interest in Trump's push for new limits on their arsenals. The same day, Taiwan's defence ministry said two Russian Tu-95 bombers entered its airspace.

Europe claims a seat at Ukraine talks after the Shenzhen snub

Some 40 delegations met in Berlin on Thursday; Friedrich Merz called Shenzhen a "photo opportunity" and expects new peace talks soon with Europe at the table beside the US, Russia and Ukraine. Calling in from Air Force One, Trump questioned Europe's billions in aid and said Moscow was ready to negotiate. Jared Kushner said Trump is open to a guarantee for Ukraine modelled on NATO's mutual-defence clause, and Emmanuel Macron urged allies to "reopen the question" of membership. Putin invited European leaders to Moscow but ruled out an energy or Black Sea truce after a third Ukrainian strike in four days on the Crimean Bridge. Berlin wants future aid increases locked into an EU budget framework no single capital can veto.

Commerce cleared 879,000 AI chips for Chinese buyers in six months

Documents released by House Republicans show the Commerce Department approved sales of more than 879,000 Nvidia and AMD AI chips to Chinese companies in six months, including firms on the Pentagon's list of alleged military-backed groups. The Democratic Congress that sits in early 2027 is expected to push back on Trump's accommodating China policy.

World Court finds Myanmar guilty of genocide against the Rohingya

Nine of the International Court of Justice's 15 judges found that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya in its 2017 crackdown, the first time an international court has held a state responsible for genocide. The Gambia filed the case seven years ago; the campaign drove more than 700,000 Rohingya into Bangladesh, and 1.2 million now live in camps there. The court said words of Min Aung Hlaing, now president, could have contributed to the genocide, and its test for intent bears on the pending case against Israel over Gaza. India, which has kept formal ties with the junta since the 2021 coup and backed its elections, is left exposed.

Kenya hands Mrima Hill rare earths to a US consortium

Frontier Minerals, an American consortium, won the contract for Mrima Hill, a Kenyan deposit of critical minerals estimated at more than $60bn, beating Chinese and Australian bidders. China runs over 90% of rare-earth mining, refining and magnet-making, and its curbs on yttrium cut sales to one buyer by 98%. In Washington the incoming Congress is examining the administration's dozen-plus magnet deals, worth up to $2.2bn each: USA Rare Earth's $277m package was placed by Cantor Fitzgerald, run by Howard Lutnick's sons, and 1789 Capital, where Donald Trump Jr is a partner, invested in Vulcan Elements months before its $620m conditional loan.
