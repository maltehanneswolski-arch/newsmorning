Person

Donald Tusk

Poland · b. 1957

Prime Minister of Poland 2023- · President of the European Council 2014-19 · Prime Minister of Poland 2007-14

A Gdańsk historian by training and a Solidarity-era activist who co-founded Civic Platform in 2001. He is the only Pole to have led both his own government and the European Council, and he returned to the premiership in December 2023 at the head of a coalition running from the centre-left to the agrarian right.

Why it matters. His government suspended two senior justices of the constitutional court and moved to bring criminal charges against them, pushing the argument with President Karol Nawrocki over who controls the bench into confrontations on the street in Warsaw.

Barred from academic work under martial law, he spent part of the 1980s earning a living as a high-altitude window cleaner on Gdańsk tower blocks in a co-operative of blacklisted Solidarity activists.

Institution

Trybunał Konstytucyjny

Constitutional Tribunal of Poland · Warsaw

Rules on the constitutionality of statutes and ratified treaties · settles competence disputes between central state bodies

Fifteen judges elected by the Sejm for single nine-year terms with no renewal. Its 2021 judgment that parts of the EU treaties are incompatible with the Polish constitution opened the confrontation with Brussels over the primacy of European law, and the composition of the bench has been disputed since the contested appointments of 2015.

Why it matters. Control of the court is the object of the fight between the Tusk government and President Nawrocki: its chief judge was removed this week, two justices were suspended, and riot police were deployed outside the building.

Created by a constitutional amendment in 1982 and sitting from 1986, it was one of very few constitutional courts to operate in a Soviet-bloc state, although the Sejm could initially overturn its rulings.

Riot police outside the Polish constitutional court

Poland's government suspended two senior justices of the constitutional court, moved to bring criminal charges against them and removed its chief judge, and the standoff ended with officers deployed outside the building in Warsaw. The dispute runs between Donald Tusk and President Karol Nawrocki over who may appoint to the bench, and neither office can finish it alone: the president does not command the Sejm, and the prime minister does not command the presidency. That court has repeatedly found for Polish statute over European law, so the composition of its bench decides how much of the EU acquis is justiciable inside Poland. Removing judges by administrative act answers a contested appointment with a contested removal, and that symmetry is what critics in the European Parliament will press whatever the merits of the individual cases. Brussels has no fast instrument here either, since the Article 7 route needs a unanimity that has never existed and budget conditionality reaches money, not benches.

Pyongyang takes 6.36 billion dollars from Binance

The theft of 6.36 billion dollars from Binance is the largest on record, and researchers have attributed it to the North Korean unit tracked as Trader Traitor. Roughly half the sum has been frozen, co-chief executive Richard Teng has committed to covering every loss, and the US Secret Service says it is working to recover the remainder. The intrusion began at an employee endpoint inside an exchange that said in 2024 it held more than 160 billion dollars in assets under custody. In Atlanta the same week the foreign ministers of South Korea, the United States and Japan pledged full implementation of Security Council resolutions, naming the ban on revenue that supports the nuclear and ballistic missile programmes. A sanctions architecture built around banks and shipping has no purchase on that kind of access, so attribution is doing the work interdiction cannot.

Ottawa leaves Montreal with a date rather than a definition

The 21st EU-Canada summit closed in Montreal on 30 October with a joint statement and an undertaking to define the future partnership by 2027, which converts an open-ended courtship into a deadline. Mark Carney has endorsed the deepest possible partnership short of membership while leaving its price and the remaining trade differences unsettled, and associate membership has no basis in the treaties to fall back on. Whatever the two sides write by 2027 therefore has to be assembled from instruments that already exist, starting with CETA and third-country participation in Union programmes. The pending definition suits Ottawa, which can keep pricing its European option against Washington for as long as the file stays open. Precedent is the harder problem for Brussels: a bespoke tier built for Canada becomes the template every other close partner cites, and the EU-UK summit falls on 20 November.

The plenum spends its communique on names

The fifth plenum of the 20th Central Committee closed on 29 October with a resolution on full and rigorous party self-governance, and fixed the 21st Party Congress for the second half of 2027. Personnel carried what the text did not: one Central Military Commission vice-chairman, Zhang Youxia, was expelled from the party among 33 expulsions, while the other, Zhang Shengmin, who ran the military anti-graft purge, was elected to the Politburo. Sixteen generals and defence-industry executives were named, among them former theatre commanders and heads of the CMC General Office, and ten alternates moved up to full membership, including the governors of Zhejiang and Shanxi. Attendance came to 162 full members and 117 alternates, 279 in all against 315 a year earlier. Analysts expect military representation in the top leadership to be rebuilt at the congress, so the purge is better read as clearing ground for a reconstruction than as an end state.

Washington widens the capital controls before the summits

Treasury proposed regulations on 22 October implementing the COINS (Comprehensive Outbound Investment National Security) Act that would carry the outbound investment regime well past its China-only origins, extending it to Iran, North Korea, Russia and Cuba. The covered technology list grows with it, picking up high-performance computing and hypersonics alongside semiconductors, quantum and AI, and Treasury is using its designation authority to reach categories the original framework did not. For AI the proposal drops the tiered structure in favour of prohibiting covered US transactions with foreign entities developing qualifying systems, which turns a notification regime into a ban. Beijing replied on the adjacent file, condemning the Federal Communications Commission decision to bar device testing inside China by 2028 in a statement issued on Friday night, days after defending its own export restrictions on AI technology. The timing is the instrument: both sides are building leverage before the Xi-Trump meetings at APEC and the G20, and European firms that take US capital and sell into China inherit a compliance exposure that no Union measure currently mediates.

Interceptors approved, and a truce that moves people

Washington cleared a 2.1 billion dollar sale of 500 Patriot interceptors to Kyiv, the only munition that reliably brings down Russian ballistic missiles, and Volodymyr Zelensky called the decision strong while pressing for the rounds to be handed over now instead of on a schedule. Brussels has meanwhile set out roughly 45 billion euros of lending for next year, with more than two thirds earmarked for arms manufacturing, and has already withheld 265 million euros over reforms Kyiv missed. Those two figures point the same way, towards financing that buys output instead of budget stability, and that makes Ukrainian industrial absorption the binding constraint, not European willingness. On the ground a rare ceasefire held long enough to move hundreds of residents out of Russian-occupied Oleshky through Russia and Belarus, and Kherson had its first quiet days in nearly five years, with local fear that Moscow is using the pause to reinforce. New casualty figures published this week show the Kursk and Krynky operations were disasters for Ukraine, a counterweight to any reading in which interceptors by themselves change the arithmetic.

Uncrewed aircraft take over the patrolling

A GJ-21 stealth reconnaissance-strike drone completed its first take-off and landing from the Sichuan, China's first Type 076 amphibious assault ship, during sea trials late this month, giving the navy a shipborne platform for work that previously needed a carrier air wing. Tokyo has recorded more Chinese military aircraft in the passages that let Beijing's fleet move beyond the first island chain, at the same time as large joint exercises with the United States, while a US Marine detachment flies uncrewed aircraft near contested South China Sea features. The coastguard added a third layer, training with water cannons and drones around Second Thomas Shoal and Sabina Shoal against what state television called foreign infringement. Each of these lowers the political price of presence, since an aircraft lost or challenged without a crew produces neither a casualty nor a detained pilot to force an escalation decision. For European capitals drafting Indo-Pacific strategies the practical consequence is that the incident ladder in these waters now has several rungs below anything a navy would call an engagement.

The drone money lands in Riga and The Hague

Latvia and the Netherlands will host the new European Drone Technology Hubs, the Latvian centre taking air and land systems and the Dutch one maritime assets, which places the work with the two capitals closest to the threats it is meant to answer. The procurement argument runs alongside it, with Beretta's chief executive arguing that spending SAFE money outside the Union is unjustifiable while European firearms makers have the capacity, the industrial-base reading of an instrument written mainly as a financing tool. German demands have shaped the latest EU Space Act compromise, which also tightens the conditions under which American companies reach the single market. Further up, the money itself is contested: culture, media and rights organisations are fighting a proposed 30 percent cut to the 8.6 billion euros earmarked for Agora EU in the next long-term budget. A new 2 euro handling fee on parcels from outside the Union, on top of the 3 euro charge announced in June, is being presented partly as a contribution to that budget, turning a customs measure into a revenue question.

The AI trade starts pricing its own financing

OpenAI cut its funding target by 10 billion dollars and is now seeking about 20 billion at a valuation of 1.25 trillion, while reports that Anthropic could carry a junk rating after listing put the sector's borrowing costs on the table. The repricing travelled east within hours: the KOSPI closed 3.48 percent lower at 5,730.74 on a semiconductor sell-off after touching 5,599.42, and the Nasdaq now sits 5.13 percent below its 6 October high. Underneath the market the real economies have diverged, with Taiwan posting 14.36 percent third-quarter growth, a 48-year high and roughly four times South Korea's pace, on the same chip demand. Chinese technology has had none of the upside, the ChiNext index trading 38 percent below its 13 May peak on unprofitability and circular financing. Europe's exposure is indirect and still material, because the capital spending now in question is what pays for data-centre construction in the United Kingdom and elsewhere on the continent, and Europe has no domestic buyer of comparable size to take up the slack if that spending pauses.
