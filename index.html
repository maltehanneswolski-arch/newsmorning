Background on today's names

Person

Micheál Martin

Ireland · b. 1960

Taoiseach (prime minister) of Ireland

A former history teacher from Cork, he led Fianna Fáil from 2011, was Taoiseach from 2020 to 2022 and returned to the office in January 2025 after serving as foreign minister.

Why it matters. He hosts the European Political Community summit in Dublin on Thursday and the informal meeting of EU leaders on Friday, where the choice of a European envoy for the Ukraine talks is on the table.

Ireland holds the rotating presidency of the Council of the EU until Lithuania takes over in January.

Institution

European Political Community

European Political Community · Dublin

Twice-yearly forum of EU members and their neighbours, from Turkey and Armenia to the UK

Proposed by Emmanuel Macron in 2022 and first convened in Prague that October, it has no secretariat or treaty and takes no binding decisions.

Why it matters. Its Dublin meeting has become the first European gathering after the Abu Dhabi round, and Macron wants it to start setting principles for Europe's future security.

Each summit is hosted alternately by an EU member and a non-member.

Europe's seat at the Ukraine talks now hangs on Putin

Two days of US-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi ended without a breakthrough but with a prisoner swap billed as the largest of the war, and Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff flew to Moscow to see Vladimir Putin before meeting Volodymyr Zelensky in Dublin on Thursday. Kushner says Europeans joining the next round needs Putin's blessing, and that naming one person to speak for Europe would achieve more than winning Moscow's consent. Zelensky wants that envoy chosen by the end of November; Friedrich Merz prefers the current format of France, Germany, the UK and Italy, and EU leaders take it up in Dublin on Friday at a meeting first meant only for AI.

The Nvidia spy scandal reaches the chip controls

Nvidia's board has hired Wachtell Lipton and is reported to be preparing to remove Jensen Huang over his affair with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive whom US intelligence believes worked with the Ministry of State Security; the shares fell another 5.5%. Axios reports that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick stalled curbs on chip diversion that Trump approved for the third time this year on 5 November, and records show a military-linked Chinese university bought H200 chips despite the US blacklist. To keep selling to China, the tech company pays Washington 15% of its H20 and 25% of its H200 sales there, which puts its boardroom in the middle of a geopolitical fight a week before Trump meets Xi Jinping at APEC in Shenzhen.

Berlin buys its 2027 budget by dropping three tax fights

The CDU/CSU and SPD group leaders announced a deal at 23:31 on Wednesday, before Thursday's final budget committee session: the plastic tax is off for now, the sugar tax waits until 2028, and the SPD reversed planned cuts to child maintenance advances, the low-income child supplement and housing benefit. Merz's promised electricity tax cut, which needs €5.4bn, is nowhere in the committee draft. The German Council of Economic Experts warned the same day that moving debt-financed defence spending back into the regular budget would open a gap of about €300bn by 2040, and wants pension reform and an end to tax breaks on diesel and restaurants.

Tuition fees hold up the EU-UK summit declaration

EU ambassadors failed on Tuesday to agree a draft statement for the 20 November EU-UK summit, because Italy, Hungary, Slovenia, Spain, Romania, Slovakia and Bulgaria want the packages on emissions trading, food trade and youth mobility tied to a UK promise of home-fee status for EU students. The draft gives both sides six months to settle fees, and ambassadors meet again next Wednesday. A carbon-market deal is close, London is courting access to Made in Europe, and both sides are set to delay a 10% tariff on electric cars that European carmakers said would expose them to Chinese competition.

Tehran threatens US bases in Europe

Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei said on Wednesday that Tehran would strike US bases in Europe if they were used for raids on Iran, on the day the US justice department charged three men over plots for the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) and Kataib Hezbollah, including a September incident near RAF Fairford. Scott Bessent, who runs the sanctions drive, has moved to a military base after an alleged Iranian threat, and Washington's plan to close Iran's land borders depends on neighbours that fear the economic fallout. Iran says a tanker hit a mine in Hormuz after the US military declared the strait cleared, and Israel's security cabinet has ordered preparations for a possible attack.
