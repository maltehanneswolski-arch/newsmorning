Washington files overcapacity tariffs on 14 economies

The US Trade Representative on Wednesday proposed tariffs on 14 economies, including the EU and China, after a months-long probe into excess industrial capacity, nine months after the Supreme Court struck down the reciprocal tariff regime. Combined rates run up to 15% for the EU, Japan and South Korea, in line with their deals, 18% for Bangladesh and Indonesia, 20% for China, 25% for India (17% with a deal) and 35% for Vietnam (20% with a deal), from the new year. Brussels rejects the premise and expects Washington to keep last year's 15% ceiling, Seoul says it will not change its industrial policy, and Trump hosts the G20 in Miami in two weeks.

Costa takes a €1.78tn budget into a summit without a broker

EU leaders open two days of talks in Brussels today on António Costa's €1.78 trillion 'nego box' for 2028-2034, with Friedrich Merz leading capitals that want sharp cuts and Paris asking for an extra €35bn for farmers to raise the cost of a future Le Pen government picking the deal apart. Merz threatens a veto unless all 27 share the €45bn loan covering Ukraine's 2027 gap; Hungary, the Czech Republic, Slovakia and Bulgaria want to opt out, and interest on that loan and the existing €90bn one could reach €55-65bn, draining the €100bn earmarked for Kyiv after 2027. Expectations of a deal before December are low, and leaders are set to confirm Pablo Hernández de Cos as ECB president over Klaas Knot.

Nvidia boards in Russian drones put chip controls back on the table

Ukrainian engineers found Nvidia Jetson Orin minicomputers guiding a Russian drone that killed three people in Zaporizhzhia and a new Russian missile, on circuit boards stamped Made in China; Nvidia says they are consumer-grade parts and that it does not sell to Russia. Trump and JD Vance lobbied Nvidia's board to reinstate Jensen Huang, and Chuck Schumer says Huang's China sales broke export law. Nvidia has now halted chip shipments to Southeast Asia as Washington tightens rules on offshore Chinese access to computing, Citigroup and Standard Chartered are vetting Singapore and Malaysia data-centre clients, and Congress is pushing new China chip curbs into the year-end defence bill.

Vox nears a share of power in Spain

Polls point to Alberto Núñez Feijóo's People's Party winning Sunday's snap election with Vox as junior partner, which would put the radical right in a Spanish government for the first time since Franco and end eight years of Socialist-led rule under Pedro Sánchez. Vox began in late 2013 as a PP breakaway, grew by confronting Catalan separatists in 2017 and has since borrowed 'remigration' and Le Pen's 'national preference', with two ideologues, Kiko Méndez-Monasterio and Gabriel Ariza, and the Disenso think tank setting the line. Young men are leading the swing to the right against their elders.

Germany's interior ministers prepare for an AfD state government

The AfD's Ulrich Siegmund could be elected minister-president of Saxony-Anhalt on 8 December, or in a third round after that, giving the party its first state government and a seat in the flow of extremism intelligence. The Länder interior ministers meet in Hamburg from Wednesday, probably for the last time without an AfD colleague; they dropped unanimity in September, so 13 states now carry a decision and a Magdeburg minister could not block one. They are weighing whether to cut Saxony-Anhalt out of the Verfassungsschutz (domestic intelligence) exchange on extremism, Islamism and hybrid threats, though one CDU minister warns that warnings from Magdeburg about Islamist attackers would then stop too. Kurti presses Brussels for Kosovo's candidate status

Albin Kurti used Wednesday's EU-Western Balkans summit to demand candidate status for Kosovo, the only Western Balkan country without it, saying the Commission had been too soft on Aleksandar Vučić and that Pristina's reforms go unrewarded. The Commission's latest scorecard faulted Kosovo for undelivered reforms, Serbia's new leaders have not moved on Kosovo's independence since the Student Movement ended Vučić's rule, and Kurti does not rule out joining Albania if the EU path closes. The summit's Brussels declaration restated the membership perspective, and Slovenia's Janez Janša pushed for tougher language on Belgrade releasing its Yugoslav-era spy archives.
