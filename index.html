Person

Mojtaba Khamenei

Iran · b. 1969

Supreme Leader of the Islamic Republic of Iran since 2026

The second son of Ali Khamenei, he is a cleric trained in Qom who spent decades inside his father's office, where he built close ties to the Revolutionary Guard and the Basij militia before succeeding him.

Why it matters. He said this week that 'the slightest inclination toward submission' would only embolden Washington, as Qatar carries a nuclear proposal from Tehran and Trump weighs new strikes.

The US Treasury put him under sanctions in 2019, years before he held any public office.

Bank of England

Bank of England · London

The UK's central bank · its Monetary Policy Committee sets Bank Rate

Founded in 1694 to lend to the government of William III, it was given operational independence over interest rates in 1997 and also supervises British banks and insurers.

Why it matters. It raised UK interest rates on Thursday for the first time in three years and warned of further hikes if the Iran war is not resolved.

Trump threatens to finish Iran as Qatar carries Tehran's nuclear offer

Trump warned on Thursday that the US may have to 'destroy' the rest of Iran and that people would 'know soon enough', and US officials reportedly told Gulf allies to prepare for new strikes. Mojtaba Khamenei answered that 'the slightest inclination toward submission' would only embolden the US, and Iran now lets ships from India, China, Qatar and Iraq through Hormuz, where five tankers and a tugboat caught fire on Thursday. Former US ambassador Ryan Crocker put the chance that a smaller air campaign wins what Operation Epic Fury did not at 'absolutely zero'.

Beijing's envoy promises countermeasures if Brussels uses its new trade weapons

Cai Run, China's ambassador to the EU, used a rare scripted speech at Friends of Europe, with printed copies handed to officials and diplomats, to reject the premise of the trade instruments Brussels is preparing against a deficit with China of about €1bn a day. Both camps are still arming: the Commission is assembling new tools, while European governments, already facing weak growth, look ill-placed to persuade voters to absorb Chinese retaliation.

The Democratic sweep wipes $2.5tn off the Nasdaq and halts trading in Seoul

The Nasdaq fell 5.5%, the S&P 500 4.4% and the Dow 3.4% on Thursday as investors priced in a Democratic Congress that could slow AI infrastructure and regulate the industry harder; Nvidia lost about 9%, erasing its gains for the year, and weak outlooks from Qualcomm and Arm added to the sell-off. Hoax bomb threats divert European flights for a second time in a week

Hoax bomb threats disrupted at least 34 European flights this week, 25 of them on Thursday alone on routes touching 16 countries from Ireland and Portugal to Finland and Serbia, with some aircraft forced into emergency landings. The alerts follow cyberattacks on supermarkets earlier in the week.
