A caretaker with no majority and a free hand

With 85 percent of the vote counted, Netanyahu’s bloc is projected at 53 seats, fifteen fewer than it held and eight short of the 61 a government needs, against 48 for Gadi Eisenkot’s opposition bloc and a record 19 for the Arab parties. Those 19 seats are the only arithmetic that removes him and the one both Jewish blocs have refused to use, which leaves a unity government that keeps him in place, a minority government propped up from outside, or a sixth election — Israel held five between 2019 and 2022. Coalition talks run for weeks, and caretaker provisions let a prime minister keep using military force while they do, so the vote left the Iran file open rather than closing it. US officials have told Middle Eastern governments to expect renewed strikes on Iran after the 3 November midterms and to prepare for Iranian retaliation against them. Brussels has parked its own sanctions discussion until a government exists, so the caretaker inherits the calendar along with the portfolio.

Montreal gets its partnership once the word is deleted

EU leaders reach Montreal today for two days with Mark Carney, having first struck out the phrase that got them there: von der Leyen’s offer of associate membership, floated in her State of the Union with Carney in the hall, was removed from the draft agreement before the talks opened, because no such status exists in a union with one tier of membership and several capitals said so. What survives is the substance — trade, joint defence and energy projects, critical raw materials, space, AI and Canadian access to Erasmus+ — and the European Parliament’s lead member on the file has said publicly that the label matters less than those chapters. The timing is deliberate: Washington is six days from its midterms, and both sides want the architecture standing before the US president turns back to it. He has called associate membership laughable and threatened to halt trade with Europe if he judges the arrangement hostile. One physical limit runs through the energy chapter, because Canada’s LNG terminals face the Pacific, so supplying Europe needs contractual workarounds rather than a shipping route.

A dozen missiles answer the offer of talks

North Korea fired about a dozen short-range ballistic missiles from the Wonsan area at around half past twelve local time on Thursday, flying roughly 250km into the East Sea, days after Seoul offered talks on easing border tensions set off by a land mine blast. The presidential Office of National Security met in emergency session and called on Pyongyang to stop. That sits awkwardly against the other signal in the file: Seoul’s National Intelligence Service told lawmakers that Kim Jong Un may have conveyed his position on possible talks with Washington during his recent visit to China. On the alliance side the news was a postponement, with Defence Minister Kang Shin-chul and Pete Hegseth, meeting for the first time on the margins of the ASEAN defence ministers’ gathering in the Philippines, pushing the annual Security Consultative Meeting into early 2027 and substituting lower-level talks in Washington next month. The delay matters because that meeting is where a date would be set for transferring wartime operational control — the authority to command South Korean forces in a war — and the ruling party’s patience with waiting has begun to show.

Two central banks, one inflation problem and a French bond market

The Federal Reserve held its policy rate at 3.75 to 4.0 percent on Wednesday despite rising inflation, with Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack dissenting in favour of an increase and markets now pricing one for December. Chair Kevin Warsh is reshaping the calendar as well, cutting 2027 to seven rate decisions from eight. Christine Lagarde has the harder sitting today: euro-area inflation is at a three-year high while French ten-year yields have passed five percent for the first time in 24 years, which makes tightening expensive for precisely the member state least able to absorb it. Her 2020 remark that the ECB is not there to close spreads has aged into a question she will be asked rather than a position she can restate. The quarter has meanwhile been generous to the banks, with Deutsche Bank’s third consecutive record quarter at just over €2.6bn before tax, BNP Paribas up 13.5 percent to almost €3.5bn alongside a €1.3bn buyback and UBS up eight percent at $2.7bn, and Christian Sewing attributes the German corporate share of it to the federal spending package reaching real business.

A toll booth at Hormuz, and an oil price that believes it

Houthi forces claimed a strike on Ras Tanura, Saudi Arabia’s main crude export terminal, on Tuesday, and said they had repelled an advance near the Bab-el-Mandeb strait. Tehran’s announcement that it will start going after tankers which have not paid its Strait of Hormuz transit charges turns a risk into a declared policy, and that is a different thing for insurers and charterers to price than an incident. Airlines are already adjusting: with jet fuel at 30 to 40 percent of operating costs, European and Asian carriers that historically hedged more of their fuel than US rivals are pulling back from hedging and warning on fares, which is what capitulating to a price level looks like. Inside Iran, Tehran has contracted private developers to repair and replace tens of thousands of damaged buildings during the truce, which reads as preparation for the next round rather than confidence in this one. For European energy planning the duration matters more than the spot price, because the longer a toll regime at Hormuz holds, the more it hardens into a standing cost of Gulf supply instead of a war premium.

The court asks why enrolled voters must apply to stay enrolled

The Supreme Court has challenged the Election Commission of India over the Special Intensive Revision of the electoral rolls, and specifically over its demand that people already on the rolls re-apply using Form 6 whenever officials failed to reach them in person. The bench also said it had never approved the Commission’s use of logical discrepancies, having allowed it only as an interim measure in the West Bengal case, which reopens the deletion of 27 lakh — 2.7 million — voters in that state. It pressed the Commission on a further 47 lakh people listed as absent, shifted, duplicate or deceased, and asked why they were not offered the chance to file an enumeration form. Outside the courtroom the Commission has let a 30-day statutory deadline lapse on a transparency request for the recorded dissents of two Election Commissioners, who have objected to the revision 14 times in 10 months. The opposition INDIA bloc, refused several Delhi venues, will stage a public impeachment of the chief election commissioner at its president’s residence ahead of a rally on 1 November.

Brussels drafts the instruments, industry drafts the objections

European industry is pushing back against Commission plans to compel supply-chain diversification away from China, ahead of a package due in December. Diversify EU is the instrument with teeth, because an obligation on firms in designated sectors to widen their supplier base is a purchasing decision taken out of a company’s hands, and the objections are loudest where substitution is slow and expensive. It arrives while the Commission is also struggling to place the second tranche of its SAFE defence-loan scheme, with €21bn still looking for takers and tighter procurement rules making the round a harder sell. A separate equity pot for frontier technology remains at the exploration stage, so the offensive half of the December package is less advanced than the defensive half. What decides the cost is which sectors get designated, since that is the point where a reporting duty becomes a purchasing duty and the bill lands on identifiable firms.

North Korean workers reach Russian factories as students

An investigation has traced an online recruitment pipeline advertising North Korean labour directly to Russian factories, with workers entering as students to get around the sanctions that bar their employment. Both governments are described as backing the network, which makes it a state arrangement rather than a grey market and removes the usual deniability about enforcement. The money runs two ways that matter: wages return to Pyongyang as hard currency, and the labour itself fills gaps in an industrial base running a war economy. This is the economic layer of a relationship that has widened militarily and politically since the two leaders renewed their cooperation pledge at a summit in 2023, and it is the layer least covered by existing sanctions instruments, which were written for state contracts rather than for job adverts. The gap for European sanctions policy is enforcement rather than design, because the prohibition already exists and the workers are already there.

Frameworks handed out along the first island chain

Pete Hegseth used the ASEAN defence ministers’ meeting in Cebu to propose a US-ASEAN defence framework and to pledge extra military cooperation funding for the Philippines together with work on interoperability, disclosing neither the amount nor a timetable for either. Japan and the United States separately agreed to build a facility in Japan for joint production of the advanced medium-range air-to-air missiles carried by F-35s, which moves the alliance from buying munitions to making them. Taiwan’s national security council secretary-general published a map locating about 50 Chinese vessels across regional waters, the carrier Liaoning among them, and called the threat obvious. Australia’s defence chief described the Chinese military build-up as creating pressure in Southeast Asia, the same assessment in a flatter register from a country that rarely puts it on the record. The pattern is architecture without numbers, with frameworks, facilities and declarations announced faster than the funding lines that would make them real, which is the gap to watch when European partners are asked to contribute to any of it.
