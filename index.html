Know the players

Background on today's names

Person

Ilse Aigner

Germany · b. 1964

President of the Bavarian Landtag and the CDU-CSU candidate to succeed Frank-Walter Steinmeier as federal president

A CSU politician from Upper Bavaria, she served as federal agriculture and consumer protection minister from 2008 to 2013, then as Bavaria's economy minister, before taking the chair of the state parliament in 2018.

Why it matters. The CDU leadership nominates her today; with the SPD and the Greens also signalling support, she is on course to become Germany's first woman president at the Federal Convention in late January.

She trained as a radio and television technician before going into politics.

Institution

Bundestag

German Bundestag · Berlin

Germany's federal parliament · its 630 members make up half of the Federal Convention that elects the president

Elected every four years, it chooses the chancellor and passes federal law, and it has sat in the Reichstag building since 1999.

Why it matters. Its members and an equal number of state delegates form the Federal Convention that votes on Ilse Aigner in late January; she meets the SPD and Green groups there on Tuesday.

A 2023 electoral reform capped the chamber at 630 seats, down from 736.

Europe heads to the Abu Dhabi talks without knowing who will speak for it

Russia and Ukraine meet under US mediation in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday and Wednesday, the first three-way round since February, with Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner for Washington. Merz backs Ilse Aigner to become Germany's first woman president

The CDU leadership nominates Ilse Aigner today to succeed Frank-Walter Steinmeier, after Friedrich Merz confirmed his backing at a presidium dinner on Sunday evening. The Bavarian Landtag president would be the first woman and the first CSU member in the office, and with the SPD and the Greens signalling support, the majority in the Federal Convention at the end of January is secure. Merz was among the last senior figures to back her, months after the two discussed the post at Tegernsee; she meets the SPD and Green groups on Tuesday, has no invitation from the AfD or Die Linke, and plans no campaign tour.

China nears high-income status with its savings glut still exported

China's gross national income per head is US$145 short of the World Bank's high-income threshold, and it is on course to cross it in 2027. A national saving rate of 42.7% of GDP and household consumption below 40% since 2005 spill into a current-account surplus that reached a record US$735bn in 2025, or 3.7% of GDP, and partners answer with trade restrictions that narrow Beijing's options further. The second EU-China Trade and Investment Consultations on 8-9 October left subsidies, the Foreign Subsidies Regulation and export controls unresolved, and Beijing refused the Commission a general licence for rare earths and permanent magnets.
