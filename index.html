Background on today's names

Person

Pablo Hernández de Cos

Spain · b. 1971

General Manager of the Bank for International Settlements and ECB president-designate

An economist who spent his career at the Bank of Spain, he was its governor from 2018 to 2024 before moving to Basel to run the Bank for International Settlements.

Why it matters. EU leaders' largest countries backed him over the Dutch candidate Klaas Knot to succeed Christine Lagarde, making him the first Spaniard to lead the ECB.

He chaired the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision from 2019 to 2023.

Bank for International Settlements

Bank for International Settlements · Basel

The bank owned by the world's central banks, which hosts their cooperation and the Basel banking standards

It acts as a bank for central banks, holds part of their reserves and hosts the committees that set international rules for bank capital and payment systems.

Why it matters. Its general manager, Pablo Hernández de Cos, has been chosen to be the next president of the European Central Bank.

It was founded in 1930 to handle German reparation payments under the Young Plan.

Frugal walkout sinks Costa's budget summit

The special summit on the nearly €2 trillion budget for 2028-2034 ended after one day, shortly after midnight, when Denmark, Austria, Sweden and Belgium refused to join António Costa's small-group huddles and Sweden's prime minister boycotted a meeting with him. Costa's €204bn compromise satisfied neither the frugals, who want deeper cuts, nor the Friends of Cohesion, who want farm and regional money spared. Emmanuel Macron says he will not negotiate past year-end, since talks running into France's May election could mean dealing with Marine Le Pen, but Giorgia Meloni, Rob Jetten and Luxembourg's Luc Frieden rejected the deadline. Costa is drafting a new negotiating box, and leaders meet again on 17 December.

Merz ties the budget to Kyiv's €45bn loan

Hungary, Slovakia and Czechia, three member states, still refuse to share the guarantees behind the EU's €45bn loan to Ukraine for 2027, and Friedrich Merz has threatened to veto the budget deal unless the two files are linked. Bulgaria, which first sided with the holdouts, is open to a compromise in which it would cover only the costs of the loan's non-military parts, and Merz sees signs that this could break the resistance. The Friends of Cohesion group has swung behind the new loans, a possible trade for smaller cuts to regional spending. Berlin also wants the Ukraine funding locked in before a possible far-right French presidency.

Hernández de Cos wins the ECB presidency for Spain

Pablo Hernández de Cos, head of the Bank for International Settlements and a former Bank of Spain governor, will succeed Christine Lagarde as ECB president after the EU's largest countries backed him over the former Dutch central bank chief Klaas Knot; he will be the first Spaniard to run the bank. A Merz-Macron arrangement had already secured the chief economist's post for Germany's Markus Brunnermeier, the familiar pairing of a northern hawk with a southern dove. The Spanish win also helps Ireland's Phil Hogan, who has Berlin's backing for the EU nomination to lead the UN Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), against Spain's Luis Planas and Italy's Maurizio Martina.

Spain votes on Sunday with Vox in reach of government

About 39 million Spaniards vote on Sunday in the snap election Pedro Sánchez called after protests over housing and parliament's rejection of two housing decrees; polls put the People's Party ahead of his Socialists, with Vox third and the likely coalition partner. A PP-Vox government would change Madrid's line from climate to data centres, and the two parties' record in flood-hit Valencia offers a preview. In the Council, a Spanish change of course on nicotine rules could hand opponents of tighter controls a blocking minority. Vox is gaining among young men on cost-of-living and anti-immigrant messages.
