Yuri Ushakov

Russia · b. 1947

Foreign-policy aide to the President of Russia

A career diplomat, he was Russia's ambassador in Washington until 2008 and has been Vladimir Putin's foreign-policy aide in the Kremlin since 2012.

Why it matters. He gave the Russian readout of Putin's one-on-one with Trump in Shenzhen: Ukraine was the main topic, and Putin's mood afterwards was "simply excellent".

Between Washington and the Kremlin he served as deputy head of the government staff while Putin was prime minister.

IEA

International Energy Agency · Paris

Intergovernmental energy security and forecasting agency

Set up in 1974 after the Arab oil embargo to coordinate emergency oil stocks among importing countries, it now publishes the reference forecasts on oil, gas and electricity.

Why it matters. On Thursday it called the crisis set off by the US and Israeli strikes on Iran in February the largest threat to energy security in history, with Hormuz blocked and consumers absorbing the cost.

Members must hold oil stocks equal to at least 90 days of their net imports.

Merz gathers nearly 40 delegations to fill Ukraine’s €70bn gap

Friedrich Merz hosts the coalition of the willing at the Chancellery on Thursday afternoon: nearly 40 delegations, about 30 at head-of-state or government level, plus Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. The aim is Ukraine's financing for next year, with €45bn carried by all EU members through the Commission's new loan and €25bn by other partners, repaid from frozen Russian central bank assets if Moscow pays no reparations. Berlin also wants future increases locked into an EU budget framework that no single country can veto. Hungary and Slovakia want to opt out, and the two Ukraine loans could cost about €40bn in debt service over the next seven-year budget.

Seoul and Beijing conclude a services and investment upgrade to their FTA

Lee Jae Myung and Xi Jinping declared in Shenzhen on Wednesday the substantial conclusion of talks on the second phase of their free trade agreement, begun eight years and eight months ago. It covers entertainment, tourism, legal services, retail, construction and finance, bans certain technology-transfer demands on investors, protects some activity before an investment is made and stops governments reversing some future openings. Seoul says it won most-favoured-nation treatment for Korean films and games, and K-pop stocks rallied on hopes that Beijing eases its content ban. Services trade was $38.8bn in 2024 against $272.9bn in goods; drafting, legal review and signature remain.

Washington ties a week of outages to the rush to patch AI-found flaws

Within a week, US air traffic control, Microsoft's office software, systems at Chase and Apple and an internal NSA network went down; in Europe, supermarket freezers heated instead of cooling and the universities of Leipzig and Tübingen were hacked. Microsoft fixed 1,400 vulnerabilities in October against 170 a year earlier, and a researcher found a hole in Java with OpenAI's Codex in about an hour. Claudia Plattner, head of Germany's BSI cyber agency, speaks of a new era in cybersecurity.

Iran strikes a US base in Kuwait after a US raid on Qeshm island

Kuwait said it repelled its first Iranian attack in two months, aimed at a US air base, and Bahrain was hit again after a US air strike on the Iranian island of Qeshm on Tuesday. Tehran says its strikes, including those on US facilities in Bahrain on 13 and 15 November, answer US drone surveillance and an attack on Iranian communications towers. The International Energy Agency said on Thursday that the crisis set off by the US and Israeli strikes in February is the largest threat to energy security in history, with Iran's blockade of Hormuz pushing costs onto consumers. Iraq has resumed pumping oil to Turkey through a line shut since 2014 that can carry up to 750,000 barrels a day.
