Europe sends a seven-seat team to the Ukraine talks

EU leaders in Dublin agreed on Friday that the E5 (Germany, France, Britain, Italy and Poland) plus the European Commission and the Council will represent Europe at future Ukraine negotiations, instead of a single envoy. A senior diplomat said sending Kaja Kallas was “just not possible”, and the Kremlin says Europeans can join only once they show “readiness to contribute”. Russian negotiators have revived the demand that NATO retrench as the price of a deal; Kallas told leaders not to walk into Russia's traps on European security. The Abu Dhabi round produced a prisoner exchange and no ceasefire.

Merz ties his EU budget vote to Ukraine money from all 27

Friedrich Merz says Germany will veto a deal on the 2028-2034 EU budget unless all 27 governments help fund Ukraine from 2028. Hungary, Czechia, Slovakia and Bulgaria call it blackmail, and Robert Fico says he will use Slovakia's own veto against any budget carrying Ukraine financing. All four belong to the 17-nation friends of cohesion, for whom cohesion money is worth about 10% of GDP, so loans to Kyiv backstopped by the EU budget could become the price of keeping it if Berlin drops its demand for deep cuts. At home, net new borrowing in the federal core budget rises to almost €123 billion, and the coalition committee has until Tuesday to close the remaining gaps.

Bessent blames Commerce for the slow move on Megaspeed

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on Friday blamed Howard Lutnick's Commerce Department for delaying the blacklisting of Megaspeed, the Nvidia customer suspected of diverting advanced chips to China, a day after Jensen Huang resigned over his relationship with its former executive Alice Huang. Senate Banking chair Tim Scott and three House committee chairs want Huang to testify. Megaspeed passed from China's 7Road to a British Virgin Islands entity in March 2023 and then to a Singapore holding company, the layering the 'ultimate parent' licence rule cannot see through. Trump will not raise the case with Xi in Shenzhen, and Chinese analysts float tighter rare-earth controls if Washington escalates.

Beijing extends the tariff truce with a $1 trillion surplus

China's trade surplus reached $1.04 trillion in the first ten months of 2026, with exports up about 23% in dollar terms on integrated circuits (up 111%), computers and parts (61%) and vehicles (52%). Its October surplus with the EU, $33.4 billion, was larger than the one with the United States. On 9 November Beijing extended its suspension of the extra 24% tariff on US goods to 10 January 2027, together with pauses on port fees for US-linked ships and some critical-mineral export controls, and the new US-China Board of Trade met for the first time on Friday.
