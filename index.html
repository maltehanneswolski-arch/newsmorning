Background on today's names

Person

JD Vance

United States · b. 1984

Vice President of the United States since January 2025 · former senator for Ohio

A Marine Corps veteran of the Iraq war and Yale Law graduate, he wrote the 2016 memoir Hillbilly Elegy before winning an Ohio Senate seat in 2022 and joining Trump's ticket two years later.

Why it matters. He blamed the Iran war and the fuel prices it drove for the Republican rout, the sharpest public break with Trump of his vice-presidency, as he weighs a 2028 run.

He is the first vice president born in the 1980s.

Institution

IRGC

Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps · Iran

Iran's ideological armed force, separate from the regular army · its navy patrols the Gulf and the Strait of Hormuz

Founded by decree in May 1979 to protect the revolution, it reports to the Supreme Leader rather than the government and runs its own ground, naval, aerospace and Quds forces alongside a large business empire.

Why it matters. Its navy controls passage through Hormuz, and it said this week that two tankers and a tugboat caught fire after taking an unauthorised route.

In 2019 the US listed it as a foreign terrorist organisation, the first time Washington applied that label to part of another state's armed forces.

Vance blames the Iran war for the rout, and Trump says he would fight it again

Vice President JD Vance said the Iran war and the fuel prices it drove cost Republicans both chambers of Congress; Trump called that largely true but said he would launch the war again. Once the new Congress sits early next year, Democrats will chair the appropriations committees that write military and foreign-aid bills, and their leaders pledged before the vote to cut funding for the war. War powers stay with the White House: in Foreign Affairs, Daniel Drezner and Elizabeth Saunders name Iran and Cuba as the places where a president who never faces voters again could do most damage, since military action against either needs only his say-so.

Brussels uncouples Moldova from Ukraine on the road to membership

The Commission's 2026 enlargement package says Moldova can close accession talks by early 2028, while Ukraine, which applied alongside it in 2022, can at best finish by the end of 2028 even if reforms speed up. Enlargement commissioner Marta Kos called Moldova the best performer in its class despite Russian drones, cyberattacks and recruited saboteurs, but gave no entry date for Ukraine, whose first chapter closures hinge on an interim benchmark assessment report (IBAR) in the first half of 2027 and on all 27 capitals opening the last two clusters by year-end. Georgia gets no new recommendations at all after a year of democratic backsliding.

Net payers tie Ukraine's €70bn to the fight over the EU budget

EU leaders want a deal on the next seven-year budget (MFF) and a way to raise €70bn for Ukraine by the end of the year, and Germany, Sweden and the Netherlands now use the second to press the 17-member cohesion group that defends regional and farm spending. Hungary, Slovakia, the Czech Republic and Bulgaria have ruled out another loan, the Commission is preparing options that lean towards one, and it warns that Kyiv's IMF programme depends on fresh EU money. Not every net payer wants the link: Finland's European affairs minister Joakim Strand says Kyiv's money cannot wait for a budget deal that may slip into spring. NATO hosts talks on Ukraine's security guarantees today, a day after a Brussels meeting on its post-war Prosperity Plan.
