Know the players

Background on today's names

Person

Vladimir Putin

Russia · b. 1952

President of the Russian Federation

A former KGB officer who ran the FSB before becoming prime minister in 1999, he has been president or prime minister ever since and ordered the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022.

Why it matters. He sits down with Xi Jinping and Donald Trump in Shenzhen on Thursday, the week the EU adds 137 names, and the foreign oil traders who keep his revenue flowing, to its sanctions list.

He served as a KGB officer in Dresden, in East Germany, in the late 1980s.

Institution

ASEAN

Association of Southeast Asian Nations · ten members

Regional consultation by consensus · no supranational authority

Works by convening and facilitating rather than deciding, with a small Jakarta secretariat and a chair that rotates annually. Consensus means any one member can stop anything.

Why it matters. Its Manila summit draws Donald Trump on Tuesday, and its talks with China on a South China Sea code of conduct are still short of a full text.

The secretariat's budget is split in equal shares between all ten members, so Indonesia contributes the same as Brunei.

Xi hosts Trump and Putin for a first three-way summit on Thursday

Beijing confirmed on Monday that Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin will meet in Shenzhen on 19 November on the sidelines of the APEC (Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation) summit, the first time the three nuclear powers sit down in this format. Trump says he wants it to be about nuclear weapons and denuclearisation rather than Ukraine, and the US and Russia have had no binding limits on their strategic arsenals for almost a year. Washington and Beijing are still haggling over a memorandum on military crisis communication, and Seoul is watching Workers' Party secretary Jo Yong-won, who has been in Moscow since Saturday.

EU readies 137 Russia listings that reach Gulf oil traders

The next EU package adds 137 individuals and entities to a blacklist of more than 4,600 names, among them 49 Russian banks, the insurer Balance Insurance and Tatyana Kim, Russia's richest woman. France and Germany call the listings a done deal. Foreign ministers meet in Brussels today to weigh Russia's maximalist demands from last week's Abu Dhabi talks, which have cast doubt on any negotiation that includes Europe.

China's October slowdown revives the case for more state borrowing

Economic activity slowed more than expected in October, and Beijing has pushed to speed up budget spending to make up for weaker growth. Xu Gao, chief economist at Bank of China International, argues that China's problem is too little debt rather than too much: excess savings and weak domestic demand make investment-led growth sustainable even as returns decline. In his reading, a government that tightens when demand is short ends up cutting its own revenue, which makes expansion the answer.
